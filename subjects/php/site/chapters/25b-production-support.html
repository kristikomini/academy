Production support as a process

"Disponibilità ad attività di manutenzione ed assistenza" is in the advert because it is a large part of the job and a hard one to staff: it is interrupt-driven, the code is usually somebody else’s, and half the skill is not technical at all. Treating support as a process rather than as an emergency is the difference between a developer who dreads the phone and one who becomes the person clients ask for.

From the advert

"Disponibilità ad attività di manutenzione ed assistenza". Read it honestly: you will be interrupted, you will work in code you did not write (ch. 16b), and you will sometimes talk to the customer yourself. Companies value candidates who say yes to this without flinching, because most say yes and then flinch.

Triage: severity before diagnosis

🧒 Simple

The first question is not "why is this broken". It is "how bad is it, and who is affected". Ten minutes deciding that saves you from spending an afternoon on a cosmetic bug while orders are failing in another window.

🎓 Professional

Severity is impact × scope, not how annoyed the person reporting it sounds — and that distinction is the entire discipline, because urgency as communicated is almost uncorrelated with severity as measured.

  • Everyone blocked, money stopping — checkout down, invoices not going out. Drop everything, tell people, fix now.
  • Some users blocked, or a workaround exists — same day, communicate the workaround immediately, because that alone converts an angry client into a waiting one.
  • Wrong but working — a misaligned column, a wrong label. Into the backlog, and say so out loud rather than leaving it silently unscheduled.

Then acknowledge before you investigate. A reply within minutes saying "l’ho preso in carico, ti aggiorno entro un’ora" costs thirty seconds and removes most of the escalation pressure. Silence is what makes a client call your manager, not the bug.

And check the obvious thing first: what changed? A deploy, a config change, a certificate expiry, a disk filling, somebody else’s API. Most incidents correlate with a change, and the deploy log is a faster hypothesis generator than reading code (ch. 30).

Reproduce before you fix

🧒 Simple

If you cannot make it happen on purpose, you cannot know you fixed it. A change that makes the error stop appearing is not the same as a fix, and the difference shows up three weeks later.

🎓 Professional

Get the exact conditions: which user, which record, which browser, what time — the timestamp is what lets you find the request in the logs (ch. 25). "Non funziona" is not a bug report, and asking three specific questions is faster than guessing: cosa stava facendo, cosa si aspettava, cosa è successo invece.

Then reproduce as close to production as you can. Most "impossible" bugs are data-dependent — a customer with no partita IVA, an order with a hundred lines, a name containing an apostrophe — and they do not exist in your seeded local database. A sanitised copy of production data is the highest-value thing a support-heavy team can own; note the GDPR obligation to anonymise it, which is also a good thing to mention in an interview.

When you find it, write the failing test first (ch. 24). It proves the diagnosis, it proves the fix, and it stops the bug coming back — and a regression test attached to a support ticket is the most convincing artefact you can leave behind.

⚠ Trap

Fixing the symptom under time pressure. Adding ?? 0 to stop a null error, or a try/catch that swallows it (ch. 07), makes the ticket close and leaves the cause in place — usually a record that should never have been written that way. A hotfix that hides a data problem means the data keeps degrading silently. If you must ship the quick fix, open the ticket for the real cause in the same hour, with the evidence attached, while you still remember it.

Hotfix, rollback, and the decision between them

🧒 Simple

Two ways out of a broken deploy: put the old version back, or push a small fix forward. Rolling back is nearly always faster and safer — unless the deploy changed the database, in which case going back may not be possible at all.

🎓 Professional

Default to rollback. It is the fastest way to stop the bleeding, and it is a decision you have already tested if your pipeline is honest (ch. 30). Diagnose afterwards, with the site working and nobody watching over your shoulder.

The exception is migrations, and it is a big one. If the deploy ran a migration that dropped a column or transformed data, the old code cannot run against the new schema and rollback is not available. This is the practical argument for expand/contract: deploy the additive schema change first (add the column, write to both), deploy the code, and remove the old column in a later release. It costs a second deploy and it keeps rollback on the table for both, which is worth far more than the tidiness of doing it in one step.

Hotfix process: branch from the production tag, not from main — main contains everything not yet released, and shipping it while fixing a fire is how a small incident becomes a large one. Smallest possible change, one reviewer even at 22:00, then merge back so the fix is not lost in the next release (ch. 26).

Feature flags deserve a mention: a flag turns "roll back the deploy" into "turn off the feature", which is seconds instead of minutes and affects only the broken thing. For a team shipping frequently it is the single best incident-reduction investment.

Try it. Not code — a runbook. Write one of these for the system you support and keep it beside the repository; it is the artefact that makes support survivable and the thing that makes you look senior when the person who normally handles it is on holiday.

# Runbook — invio fatture elettroniche allo SDI

## Sintomo tipico
"Le fatture di ieri risultano ancora 'in attesa'."

## Verifica rapida (5 minuti)
1. Coda:      `php artisan queue:monitor sdi --max=50`   → profondità
2. Falliti:   `SELECT COUNT(*), MAX(failed_at) FROM failed_jobs WHERE payload LIKE '%SdiInvia%'`
3. Worker:    `supervisorctl status sdi-worker:*`        → running?
4. Provider:  status page del provider SDI               → è un problema loro?
5. Log:       filtra per `channel=sdi` nell'ultima ora

## Cause note
| Sintomo                          | Causa                          | Azione                                   |
|----------------------------------|--------------------------------|------------------------------------------|
| Coda cresce, 0 falliti           | worker fermo dopo il deploy    | `supervisorctl restart sdi-worker:*`     |
| Falliti con 401                  | certificato/credenziali scadute| rinnovo — vedi §Credenziali              |
| Falliti con "codice destinatario"| dato cliente errato            | NON è un bug: correggere l'anagrafica    |
| Tutto fermo, provider in outage  | loro                           | comunicare al cliente, i job ritentano   |

## Escalation
- Dopo 30 minuti senza causa chiara → [ruolo: responsabile tecnico]
- Impatto fiscale (scadenza invio)  → [ruolo: amministrazione], subito

## Cosa NON fare
- Non svuotare `failed_jobs`: contiene le fatture da rinviare.
- Non rilanciare i job a mano prima di aver verificato l'idempotenza del lotto.

The post-mortem, and the customer-facing half

🧒 Simple

After it is fixed, write down what happened and what will stop it happening again — without blaming a person. And tell the client, in their language, what went wrong and what you have changed.

🎓 Professional

A blameless post-mortem for anything customer-visible: timeline, impact, root cause, what made it hard to detect, actions with owners. Blameless is not politeness — it is the only way to get accurate information, because a person who expects blame reports less. And "human error" is never a root cause: the question is why the system allowed it.

The most valuable line in any post-mortem is usually "how long before we noticed". If the answer is "the client told us", the action item is monitoring, not a code fix (ch. 25).

The customer-facing half is a real skill and it is what gets you asked for by name. Say what happened in their terms and not yours — "le fatture di ieri non sono partite", not "i job erano in failed_jobs". Say what the impact was, what you have done, and what prevents a recurrence. Never blame the client even when it was their data, and never say "strano, da me funziona". Give a next update time and keep it; an unanswered client escalates, an updated one waits.

Golden rules
  • Severity is impact × scope, not the volume of the complaint.
  • Acknowledge in minutes. Silence causes escalation, not the bug.
  • Ask what changed before reading any code.
  • Reproduce, then write the failing test, then fix.
  • Default to rollback — unless a migration made it impossible.
  • Expand/contract keeps rollback available.
  • Hotfix from the production tag, never from main.
  • Blameless post-mortem, and measure time-to-detect.

"È disponibile per attività di assistenza? Come gestisce un’urgenza?"

Say yes without hesitation and then show it is a process, which is what distinguishes you: "Sì, ci sono abituato. La prima cosa che faccio è rispondere subito e dire che l’ho preso in carico, anche prima di sapere la causa: metà delle escalation nascono dal silenzio. Poi classifico l’impatto — quante persone sono bloccate, si stanno perdendo soldi — perché l’urgenza percepita e la gravità reale spesso non coincidono. Guardo cosa è cambiato, di solito un deploy, e se il problema è quello faccio rollback subito e indago con il sito che funziona. Prima di dichiarare risolto voglio riprodurlo, e scrivo il test che fallisce, altrimenti non so se l’ho sistemato o solo nascosto. Alla fine scrivo al cliente in parole sue cosa è successo e cosa abbiamo cambiato perché non succeda di nuovo."
