Your CV, LinkedIn and this project

The advert never asks for a CV and the CV decides whether anyone reads the rest. Six seconds of a recruiter’s attention, and before that a parser that may not read two columns. This chapter is the rules the CV builder on this site lints against — every warning it produces corresponds to a paragraph below.

From the advert

Not in any advert. It is the gate in front of every advert. And unlike almost everything else in this course, it can be fixed in one evening.

The bullet: what you did, with what, and what changed

🧒 Simple

"Ho lavorato al backend" tells the reader nothing — it says you were present. Say what you built, what you built it with, and what was different afterwards.

🎓 Professional

Every bullet is action → means → result. Start with a verb in the past tense; name the technology inside the sentence rather than only in a skills list; end with the change.

Words that describe presence rather than result — ho partecipato, ho collaborato, mi sono occupato di, worked on, responsible for, involved in — are the ones the linter flags, because a bullet built only from those has said nothing testable. They are not banned; they simply cannot be the whole bullet.

Have a number wherever one honestly exists, and only where it does. "Da 4 s a 90 ms" is the difference between a claim and a fact; an invented figure is a trap you will be asked to explain. Numbers that are usually available and usually forgotten: response time before and after, rows or records handled, number of users, hours of manual work removed per week, size of the team.

Keep bullets under about two lines — roughly 220 characters. A bullet that wraps to four lines is skipped by the eye, which means the achievement inside it may as well not be there. 

✗  Ho collaborato allo sviluppo del backend di un gestionale.

✓  Sviluppato l'export ordini in PHP 8.2 con generator e coda Redis:
   da timeout a 30 s su 12.000 righe a 18 s in background, memoria costante.

✗  Mi sono occupato dell'ottimizzazione del database.

✓  Ridotto il tempo della pagina catalogo da 4,1 s a 90 ms risolvendo un N+1
   e aggiungendo un indice composito su (categoria_id, attivo).

The ATS, and the formatting that defeats it

🧒 Simple

Before a person sees your CV, software often reads it and turns it into fields. A beautiful two-column layout can arrive at the other end as scrambled text — and nobody tells you that is what happened.

🎓 Professional

An Applicant Tracking System parses the file into structured data. The failure is silent: you are not rejected, you are misread. The rules that keep you readable:

  • One column. Two-column templates — the ones that look best — are the most common cause of jumbled parsing, because the reading order is ambiguous. This is the single highest-risk formatting choice.
  • No text inside tables, text boxes, headers or footers. Contact details in a header are routinely dropped entirely, which is how a CV arrives with no email address.
  • PDF exported from a text document, never a scan or an image, and never a design tool that outlines the text. Check by selecting the text in a viewer: if you cannot select it, neither can the parser.
  • Standard section headings: Esperienza, Formazione, Competenze. Clever headings do not map to fields.
  • Dates in a consistent format — 03/2024 – oggi — on every entry.
  • No icons in place of words. A telephone glyph is not a label; write the words.
  • Name the file properly: CV_Nome_Cognome.pdf. cv_finale_2_def.pdf is read by a human before anything else.
  • Mirror the advert’s vocabulary where it is true. If it says Laravel and you have used Laravel, the word "Laravel" must appear — not only "framework PHP". This is keyword matching, not deception.
⚠ Trap

The missing GDPR line. An Italian CV should carry the authorisation to process personal data — "Autorizzo il trattamento dei miei dati personali ai sensi del Regolamento (UE) 2016/679 e del D.Lgs. 196/2003." Some Italian recruiting systems and many HR processes filter on its absence, and it costs one line. The English version of your CV conventionally omits it, which is right for a multinational and wrong for an Italian employer — so keep both documents, which is exactly what the builder’s language switch is for.

Structure, length and the six seconds

🧒 Simple

The top third of the first page is what gets read. Put the strongest thing there, and make sure a person can tell in one glance what you do, where you are, and how to contact you.

🎓 Professional

Order: name and contact (email, telephone, city, GitHub, LinkedIn — as text, not icons); a three-line summary; experience, most recent first; projects if experience is thin; education; skills; languages with CEFR levels.

The summary is where six seconds are spent, so make it specific and role-shaped: "Sviluppatore PHP con due anni su Laravel e MySQL, gestionali e integrazioni con sistemi di terze parti. Cerco un ruolo backend in zona Modena–Bologna." Three lines, no adjectives about being dynamic and results-oriented — every CV claims those and none of them are evidence.

One page as a junior, two once you have several years. If it is running long, cut the oldest thing, not the newest — and cut duties from old roles before cutting achievements from recent ones.

City matters in this market. Employers in Modena and Bologna filter on commuting distance, so put the city on the CV. If you would relocate, say so explicitly rather than leaving them to guess from an address two hundred kilometres away.

Languages in CEFR: B2, C1. "Buono" means nothing and is unverifiable; B2 is a claim they can test, which is precisely why it is worth more. English is genuinely used in this market — documentation always, sometimes clients — so it belongs on the CV honestly rated.

What to leave off: a photograph (normal in Italy, increasingly discouraged and irrelevant in software; harmless either way, so decide and move on), your date of birth, your marital status, your codice fiscale, and a skills bar chart claiming you are 80% at PHP — a number nobody can interpret and everybody discounts.

Try it. Open the CV builder on this site, paste in your current bullets, and read the warnings. Every rule it applies is one of the paragraphs above — and the four checks below are the ones that catch the most people.

LinkedIn, GitHub, and talking about this project

🧒 Simple

Recruiters search LinkedIn by keyword and open GitHub to see whether you actually write code. Both are worth an evening, and both are mostly about being findable and legible rather than impressive.

🎓 Professional

LinkedIn is a search index. The headline is the highest-value field — "Sviluppatore PHP / Laravel · Bologna" beats "Aspiring developer" because it contains the words a recruiter types. Fill the skills section with the actual technology names, set Open to work (it is visible to recruiters only, if you choose), and write the About section in the first person and in Italian for this market. Ask a former colleague or teacher for one recommendation; one real one is worth more than twenty endorsements.

GitHub. Two or three repositories that are finished beat fifteen abandoned ones. Every one that a recruiter might open needs a README that says what it does, why you built it, how to run it in two commands (ch. 28), and what you would do differently. Pin those; a green contribution graph is not the point and never was.

Talking about what you built here. This platform is a legitimate portfolio project and it is more interesting than a to-do list, provided you describe it as a system with decisions rather than as a course you completed. The things worth naming, because each opens a real technical conversation: it is framework-free and works from file://; the spaced-repetition scheduler and how mastery is computed; the service-worker cache bug that served stale chapters and why the cache name is now derived rather than remembered; and the integrity gate — eleven checks, three of which exist because the defect happened first and was invisible rather than loud.

That last one is the best story you have, and it is worth rehearsing: a gate that has never failed has not been tested, it has only been run. Explaining that you deliberately broke four things to check the gate caught them, and that one of them slipped through and forced a better check, is exactly the kind of judgement a technical interviewer is listening for (ch. 24, 38).

Golden rules
  • Action → means → result. Presence words cannot be the whole bullet.
  • A number wherever one honestly exists, and nowhere it does not.
  • One column, selectable text, no headers or tables.
  • The GDPR line on the Italian version. One line, filtered on.
  • One page as a junior. Cut the oldest, never the newest.
  • City on the CV. This market filters on distance.
  • CEFR levels. "Buono" is unverifiable; B2 is testable.
  • Mirror the advert's true words — "Laravel", not "framework PHP".

"Mi racconti un progetto di cui va fiero?"

Describe a decision and a failure, not a feature list: "Ho costruito una piattaforma di preparazione tecnica: statica, senza framework e senza build, che funziona anche aperta da file:// e offline. La scelta interessante è stata il controllo di integrità: undici verifiche che falliscono la build se un capitolo non corrisponde al manifest o se i conteggi nella documentazione non sono veri. Tre di quelle verifiche esistono perché il difetto era già successo — il service worker serviva i capitoli vecchi senza nessun errore, sembrava solo che le mie modifiche non avessero effetto. La cosa che mi sono portato via è che ho rotto quattro cose di proposito per vedere se il gate le prendeva, e una è passata: un controllo che non è mai fallito non è stato testato, è stato solo eseguito."
