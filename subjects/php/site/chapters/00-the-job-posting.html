The job posting, decoded

Five real adverts, one map. This chapter takes the requirement lines that actually appear in Emilia-Romagna PHP postings, translates each one out of recruiter Italian into the thing you will be asked to do, and points at the chapter that teaches it. Read it once now and once again the week before an interview — it is the only chapter here whose job is to tell you what to skip.

From the advert

This chapter answers the whole advert. Everything after it answers one line of one.

The five adverts this course was built from

🧒 Simple

Job adverts look like they were written by five different companies. They were — but they ask for nearly the same things, in a slightly different order, because the work is nearly the same. Learn the overlap and you have answered most of them at once.

🎓 Professional

The manifest for this course was derived in September 2026 from PHP postings for Emilia-Romagna and Milano: a Bologna web agency building client sites on Laravel, a Modena software house building gestionali, Zucchetti Axess in Bologna, and two consultancies staffing developers into client teams.

Every recurring line is quoted verbatim in the req field of the chapter that answers it, so the coverage claim stays checkable rather than asserted. 33 chapters answer an advert line. 14 carry an extra saying why they exist although nobody asked — those are the ones that decide whether you keep the job after you get it.

Translating a requirement line

🧒 Simple

A requirement line is not a description of a skill. It is a compressed description of a task somebody on that team does on a Tuesday. Unpack it back into the task and you know both what to study and what to talk about.

The words that carry the real information

🧒 Simple

Some words in an advert are decoration. Others tell you exactly how much they care. Learning which is which stops you ruling yourself out of a job you would have got.

🎓 Professional

The qualifier in front of a requirement is the requirement:

  • Gradita / costituisce titolo preferenziale — nice to have. Not having it does not filter you out; being able to say one intelligent sentence about it wins the comparison against an otherwise equal candidate.
  • Buona conoscenza — you will use it weekly and be expected not to need help.
  • Consolidata / comprovata esperienza — they want production scars, and they will ask a follow-up question designed to find out whether you have them.
  • Ottima conoscenza — frequently aspirational. Read the rest of the advert before believing it.
  • Autonomia — nobody has time to onboard you properly. This is a statement about the team, not about you.
⚠ Trap

The most expensive misreading in this market is treating the requirement list as a checklist you must complete before applying. It is a wish list assembled by combining several people’s wants, and it is normal for the person hired to match perhaps two thirds of it. If you match the consolidata lines and can talk sensibly about the gradita ones, you are a candidate. Waiting until you match everything means applying a year late to a job that was filled by somebody who did not.

What no advert says, and every interview tests

🧒 Simple

There are things nobody writes down because they assume it. Those are exactly the things that sink an interview, because you never thought to prepare them.

🎓 Professional

Four of them, in the order they usually cost people the offer:

  1. Debugging. Nobody asks "can you use Xdebug", and the answer separates two otherwise identical candidates by a factor in daily output (ch. 27).
  2. Legacy. No advert says "you will spend six months in PHP 5.6 code with no tests", and in this market a great deal of the work is exactly that (ch. 16b).
  3. Explaining your own code. The technical round is mostly you narrating a decision you made. If everything you have built was a tutorial, there is no decision to narrate (ch. 37, 38).
  4. Hosting reality. A large share of Italian PHP runs on shared cPanel at Aruba, not on Kubernetes. Knowing the constraints of the actual deployment target is a strong signal (ch. 29b).

How to use this course

🧒 Simple

Do not read it front to back like a novel. Read the chapters that answer the advert in front of you, take the test at the end of each, and let the review scheduler bring back what you got wrong.

🎓 Professional

Three passes, in this order:

  1. The core, whatever the advert says: 02, 03, 11, 12, 13, 15, 16. These are the chapters an interview cannot avoid, and mastering them makes every other chapter faster to read.
  2. The advert in your hand. Use the table above. If the posting says gestionale, add 33; if it says e-commerce, add 32; if it says WordPress, add 31.
  3. Part 8, one week out. The CV, the screening test and the interview itself. This is the highest return-per-hour part of the course and the part everyone leaves until it is too late.

The Simple / Pro switch in the top bar is a self-test, not a difficulty setting. Read the Simple box, cover the Pro box, and say the professional version out loud. If you cannot, you have not learned it yet — you have only recognised it.

Golden rules
  • A requirement line is a task, not a skill. Unpack it into the Tuesday it describes.
  • The qualifier is the requirement. Gradita and consolidata are different jobs.
  • Two thirds is a candidate. The list is a wish list, not a gate.
  • Prepare the unwritten four — debugging, legacy, explaining yourself, and where it deploys.
  • Read the advert’s chapters, not all of them. Depth on the quoted lines beats breadth on everything.

"Ha letto l’annuncio? Cosa la interessa di questa posizione?"

Answer with their words, not yours — quote one requirement line back and connect it to something you built: "Sì. Mi ha colpito la parte sull’integrazione con sistemi di terze parti, perché è esattamente quello su cui ho lavorato di più: ho scritto un’integrazione con retry e idempotency key verso un gestionale, e la parte difficile non era la chiamata HTTP ma decidere cosa fare quando risponde due volte." That sentence does three jobs at once: it proves you read the advert, it names a real difficulty, and it hands the interviewer their next question.
