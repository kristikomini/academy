E-commerce: Magento, PrestaShop, WooCommerce

E-commerce is where PHP’s market share is most concentrated, and the interesting part is not the framework — it is the domain. Cart, stock, VAT, payment, shipping and returns each have invariants that punish sloppiness with money, and a candidate who can talk about idempotent order creation and IVA rounding sounds like someone who has shipped a shop, because nobody learns those from a tutorial.

From the advert

"Gradita esperienza su piattaforme e-commerce". They mean Magento, PrestaShop or WooCommerce specifically — but the transferable half is the domain, and that is what an interviewer can actually probe in twenty minutes.

The platforms, and what choosing one costs

🧒 Simple

You can build a shop on a ready-made platform, or build one yourself. The platform gives you everything immediately and fights you when the client wants something unusual; building it yourself is the opposite.

🎓 Professional
  • WooCommerce. A WordPress plugin (ch. 31), so an enormous share of small Italian shops. Fast to launch, huge plugin ecosystem, and it inherits the wp_postmeta data model — which is precisely why a large catalogue with faceted filtering becomes slow, and why serious Woo work involves purpose-built tables or a search index (ch. 21).
  • PrestaShop. French, very common in Italy for mid-sized shops, strong multi-language and multi-currency support out of the box. Module-based, with a somewhat idiosyncratic architecture and a history of painful major upgrades.
  • Magento / Adobe Commerce. The enterprise end: EAV data model, extremely flexible, extremely heavy. Genuine specialist knowledge that is well paid and slow to acquire. If an advert names Magento, they know what they are asking for.
  • Shopware (Symfony-based, growing) and Sylius (Symfony, for custom builds) — the modern PHP options when you want a framework rather than a product.
  • Headless — Shopify or a commerce API behind your own front end. Increasingly common, and it turns the job into integration work (ch. 14b).

The judgement to show: customising a platform beyond a point costs more than building was going to. When the client’s pricing rules require overriding three core classes, you are no longer saving time — you are paying for the platform with every future upgrade. Being able to name that threshold is worth more than a preference between the platforms.

Money, IVA and the rounding that reaches the accountant

🧒 Simple

Money is never a decimal number in your code — it is a whole number of cents. And VAT has to be calculated and rounded in a specific order, or the total on the invoice will be a cent away from what the accounting software expects.

🎓 Professional

Integer minor units everywhere (ch. 03), or BCMath for anything that must not lose a fraction before rounding. A float total is a bug that shows up as an invoice one cent out — and in Italy that invoice went to the SDI and cannot be quietly corrected (ch. 33).

Italian VAT specifics you should know by name: the ordinary rate is 22%, with reduced rates of 10%, 5% and 4% (food, books, some services), and different products in one cart carry different rates — so VAT is per line, never one multiplication on the total.

Prices are shown inclusive of VAT to consumers (B2C) and typically exclusive to businesses (B2B), which means the same catalogue must render two ways and the stored price has to have a documented meaning. Getting this wrong is the most common e-commerce data-model mistake: decide whether price_cents is net or gross, write it down, and never mix.

Rounding. Round per line, then sum — not sum then round — and use half-up, which is what Italian accounting expects and what round() in PHP already does. A discount applied to the gross price and a discount applied to the net produce different totals; the invoice must be internally consistent, because the SDI validates that the sum of the lines equals the stated total.

Also in scope for a real shop: reverse charge for EU B2B with a valid VAT number (validated against VIES), OSS for B2C sales to other EU countries above the threshold, and esigibilità differita in some public-sector cases. You are not expected to be an accountant — you are expected to know these exist and to ask rather than assume.

⚠ Trap

Recalculating the price at checkout from the current catalogue. The customer added it at €19.99, the price changed while they were browsing, and they are charged €24.99 — or the invoice disagrees with the confirmation email. Snapshot the price, the VAT rate and the product description onto the order line at the moment of the order, and never join back to the product table for a historical figure. This is the same reason an invoice must be immutable: it is a fiscal document describing a moment, not a view of current data.

Stock, concurrency and the idempotent order

🧒 Simple

Two customers, one item left, both clicking at the same instant. Whatever you do, one of them must be told no — and deciding that with a read followed by a write is exactly how you sell it twice.

🎓 Professional

Overselling is a race condition, and the check-then-act shape is the bug: SELECT quantity, decide, UPDATE. Two requests interleave between the read and the write and both succeed. The fixes, in order of preference:

  • Conditional update — UPDATE giacenze SET qta = qta - 1 WHERE articolo_id = ? AND qta >= 1, then check the affected row count. One atomic statement, no lock held by you, and it is correct under any concurrency.
  • SELECT ... FOR UPDATE inside a transaction when you must read several rows and decide together — and keep the transaction short, because you are now holding locks (ch. 11).
  • Optimistic concurrency with a version column when the update spans a user think-time.

Reservation versus deduction is a business decision to surface rather than guess: does stock decrease when the cart is filled, at payment, or at shipping? Reserving at checkout with a timeout is the usual compromise, and it needs a job to release expired reservations (ch. 22).

Checkout must be idempotent. The customer double-clicks, the payment provider retries its webhook, the browser resubmits after a timeout — all three happen daily. Give the checkout an idempotency key (ch. 14b), put a unique constraint on it, and let the database reject the duplicate. The payment provider’s webhook, not the browser redirect, is the source of truth for "was this paid": the customer closes the tab before the redirect completes far more often than you would think, and an order that is paid but not recorded is the worst failure this domain has.

And never store card data. Use the provider’s hosted fields or redirect so the numbers never touch your server; that is what keeps you out of the expensive parts of PCI-DSS. In Italy expect Stripe, Nexi, Satispay, PayPal and bank transfer, plus Klarna or Scalapay for instalments.

Try it. The two versions of stock deduction. The first is what almost everyone writes; run the second and no amount of concurrency can oversell.

<?php declare(strict_types=1);

/* WRONG — check-then-act. Two requests can both pass the check. */
function deduceOversells(PDO $db, int $articoloId, int $qta): bool
{
    $st = $db->prepare('SELECT qta FROM giacenze WHERE articolo_id = ?');
    $st->execute([$articoloId]);
    $disponibile = (int) $st->fetchColumn();

    if ($disponibile < $qta) { return false; }        // ← another request runs right here

    $db->prepare('UPDATE giacenze SET qta = qta - ? WHERE articolo_id = ?')
       ->execute([$qta, $articoloId]);
    return true;
}

/* RIGHT — the condition is inside the UPDATE, so the database resolves the race.
   Zero affected rows means somebody else got there first. */
function deduce(PDO $db, int $articoloId, int $qta): bool
{
    $st = $db->prepare(
        'UPDATE giacenze
            SET qta = qta - :qta
          WHERE articolo_id = :id
            AND qta >= :qta'          // the check and the act, atomically
    );
    $st->execute(['qta' => $qta, 'id' => $articoloId]);

    return $st->rowCount() === 1;
}

/* Idempotent order creation: the unique constraint is the concurrency control.
     ALTER TABLE ordini ADD UNIQUE KEY uq_idem (idempotency_key);           */
function createOrder(PDO $db, string $idempotencyKey, array $righe): int
{
    try {
        $st = $db->prepare('INSERT INTO ordini (idempotency_key, stato) VALUES (?, ?)');
        $st->execute([$idempotencyKey, 'submitted']);
        return (int) $db->lastInsertId();
    } catch (PDOException $e) {
        if ($e->errorInfo[1] !== 1062) { throw $e; }   // 1062 = duplicate key

        // Already created — a double click or a retried webhook. Return the same order.
        $st = $db->prepare('SELECT id FROM ordini WHERE idempotency_key = ?');
        $st->execute([$idempotencyKey]);
        return (int) $st->fetchColumn();
    }
}

The catalogue: variants, performance and the rest of the domain

🧒 Simple

A product with sizes and colours is not one thing — it is a family, and each combination has its own code, price and stock. Getting that structure wrong at the start makes everything afterwards harder.

🎓 Professional

Variants. A parent product with child variants, each with its own SKU, price, stock and barcode. Attributes divide into those that define a variant (size, colour) and those that merely describe it (material). Getting this wrong — a separate top-level product per colour — breaks filtering, stock and the eventual ERP synchronisation, and it is expensive to change once there are orders.

Performance is the same three answers as everywhere else, applied to a specific shape: the category page is an N+1 waiting to happen (ch. 12); filtering by attribute belongs in a search index rather than in meta_query or a pile of joins (ch. 21); and the catalogue is read far more than written, so it caches well — with the price and stock deliberately excluded or short-lived, because a cached out-of-stock badge is a customer service problem (ch. 20).

The rest of the domain, which is where the tickets actually come from: shipping rules by weight, zone and threshold (and Italian islands are usually a separate zone); diritto di recesso — 14 days for consumer distance selling, so returns are a legal requirement and not a feature; a credit note (nota di credito) rather than editing an invoice; guest checkout with GDPR-appropriate consent; and synchronisation with an ERP that owns stock and prices and will not be changed to suit you (ch. 33).

Golden rules
  • Integer cents, never floats. One cent reaches the accountant.
  • VAT per line, rounded per line, then summed. Rates differ within one cart.
  • Decide net or gross once, write it down, never mix.
  • Snapshot price and VAT onto the order line. Never re-derive history.
  • Stock changes with a conditional UPDATE. Check-then-act oversells.
  • Checkout is idempotent, with a unique key the database enforces.
  • The webhook is the truth about payment, not the browser redirect.
  • Never store card data. Hosted fields or redirect.
