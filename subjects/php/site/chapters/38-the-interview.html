The interview

The last chapter, and the one where everything else gets converted into an offer. Three rounds, usually: a recruiter call, a technical interview, and a conversation about money and contract. Each measures something different, and the third is the one Italian candidates prepare least and lose most in — because RAL, netto and CCNL are not the same number and nobody explains that in advance.

From the advert

This chapter answers the whole advert, in reverse: everything the posting asked for is now something you have to say out loud, under mild pressure, to someone deciding whether to hire you.

"Mi parli di lei" — the ninety seconds that set the frame

🧒 Simple

The first question is not really a question. It is an invitation to decide what the rest of the conversation will be about. Prepare it, out loud, until it is ninety seconds and not five minutes.

🎓 Professional

A structure that works: where you are now → the most relevant thing you have built → why this role. Ninety seconds, in that order, ending on them rather than on you.

Two rules. First, plant hooks: mention two or three specifics you would be happy to be asked about — an integration, a performance problem you fixed, a technology in their advert. Interviewers follow the threads you offer, so you have real influence over which questions you get. Second, do not narrate your CV chronologically from school onwards; select for relevance.

On the career-change or self-taught story, which is common in this market: tell it as a decision with evidence, not as an apology. "Vengo da un altro settore, ho iniziato a programmare per [motivo concreto], e da allora ho costruito X e Y" is stronger than any amount of explaining what you were doing before. Nobody in a software house cares about the gap; they care whether you can do the work and whether you will stay.

The technical round, and the two questions you will be asked

🧒 Simple

Most technical interviews are the same handful of questions in a different order. You have already prepared them — this course is those questions — and the remaining skill is answering out loud, with an example.

🎓 Professional

The recurring set, with the chapter that answers each: interface versus abstract class (03); what is an N+1 and how do you find it (12); how do you make this query faster (11); how do you prevent SQL injection and XSS (15); how do you store a password (15); what happens between nginx and your code (01); how do you handle a slow page (20); what goes on a queue (22); merge or rebase (26); have you written tests (24).

The structure that makes any of them land: the direct answer, the mechanism, then an example from your own work. The example is what distinguishes you from someone who revised the same list last night. Two sentences of "and I hit this when…" outweigh a perfect definition.

When you do not know, say so and then say what you would do: "Non l’ho mai usato in produzione. Da quello che so è… e la prima cosa che verificherei è…". That is a good answer. Bluffing is the only genuinely fatal option, because a technical interviewer will follow up and the collapse is worse than the admission would have been.

Expect "parlami di un problema difficile che hai risolto", and prepare two stories in advance: situation, what made it hard, what you did, what happened, what you learned. A production incident is ideal — the cache serving stale content, the N+1 found in a view, the job failing silently in failed_jobs. And prepare one about a mistake you made, told without excessive self-flagellation and ending in the change you made afterwards; the question is testing self-awareness, not looking for a confession.

⚠ Trap

Criticising their stack, their architecture or their previous developer. "Ah, ancora PHP 7.4?" or "i microservizi qui sono sbagliati" ends interviews (ch. 22b). You are being assessed on whether you will be pleasant to work with on a Tuesday, and the same knowledge delivered as curiosity scores the opposite way: "vedo che siete su 7.4 — avete in programma l’aggiornamento? Mi è capitato di farne uno con Rector e un baseline PHPStan". Identical information, and now you are a colleague solving it with them.

RAL, netto and the contract

🧒 Simple

In Italy salaries are discussed as a gross annual figure, which is not what arrives in your bank account and is not divided by twelve. Knowing how the numbers relate is what stops you accepting something you did not understand.

🎓 Professional

RAL (retribuzione annua lorda) is gross annual pay, and it is the number every conversation uses. What you should know about turning it into reality:

  • Mensilità. RAL is divided by 13 or 14 monthly payments, not 12, depending on the contract. Two identical RALs can mean different monthly amounts — always ask which.
  • Netto. Roughly 55–70% of RAL depending on level, region and deductions. Do not estimate it in your head during the call; use a calculator afterwards.
  • CCNL. The national collective agreement — usually Metalmeccanico or Commercio for software companies — sets minimums, levels, holidays and notice periods. Ask which CCNL and which livello: it determines a great deal beyond the headline figure.
  • Contract type. Indeterminato (permanent), determinato (fixed term), apprendistato (apprenticeship — common for juniors, lower cost to the employer, includes training obligations and typically converts), or partita IVA — which is not employment: no paid holiday, no sick pay, no TFR, and you pay your own contributions, so a P.IVA rate must be substantially higher than the equivalent RAL to be comparable.
  • TFR accrues as deferred pay (roughly one month’s salary per year worked) and is a real part of the package.
  • Benefits with real value: buoni pasto (tax-free up to a daily limit, so worth more than the same amount in salary), welfare aziendale, health cover, and remote-working days — which for a Modena or Bologna role can be worth more than a few hundred euro of RAL.

On the number itself. When asked for your expectation, give a range with a reason: "in base a quello che vedo sul mercato per un profilo PHP con la mia esperienza in zona, mi aspetto una RAL tra X e Y — ma dipende anche da livello e smart working, quindi sono aperto a parlarne". Naming a range keeps the conversation open; refusing to answer at all reads as evasive, and naming a single low number anchors you there permanently. If you can, ask their budgeted range first — it is a normal question and many recruiters will answer it.

Try it. The questions you ask. Having five ready is the strongest signal in the room, and every one of these also tells you something you genuinely need to know before accepting.

SUL LAVORO VERO
  · Come è composta una giornata tipo? Quanto è sviluppo di nuove funzionalità
    e quanto manutenzione su codice esistente?          → il vero contenuto del ruolo
  · Su quanti progetti lavora una persona in parallelo? → agenzia o prodotto
  · Che versione di PHP e che framework, sui progetti attivi?

SUL PROCESSO
  · Come arriva il codice in produzione? Avete una pipeline, e ogni quanto rilasciate?
  · Fate code review? Come sono organizzate?
  · Come gestite le urgenze dei clienti? C'è una reperibilità?   → sa di cosa parla, ch. 25b
  · Che copertura di test avete oggi, realisticamente?

SULLA CRESCITA
  · Come è strutturato l'affiancamento nei primi mesi?
  · C'è budget per formazione o conferenze?
  · Come si valuta la crescita? Ci sono livelli o momenti definiti?

SUL CONTRATTO — da chiedere sempre, senza imbarazzo
  · Quale CCNL e quale livello? La RAL è su 13 o 14 mensilità?
  · Che tipo di contratto — indeterminato, apprendistato, determinato?
  · Quanti giorni di lavoro da remoto sono previsti?
  · Ci sono buoni pasto o welfare aziendale?

DA NON CHIEDERE al primo colloquio
  · Ferie e permessi come prima domanda
  · "Quanto si lavora davvero?" formulato come sospetto

Closing well, whatever the outcome

🧒 Simple

Ask what happens next, thank them in writing the same day, and if you are turned down, ask for one piece of feedback. The market here is small and the same people reappear.

🎓 Professional

Before leaving the room, ask two things: what the next step is and by when, and whether they have any reservations about your profile. The second is uncomfortable and occasionally decisive — it lets you answer an objection you would otherwise never have heard, and it is often a small factual gap you can close in one sentence.

Follow up in writing the same day. Four lines: thank you, one thing from the conversation that interested you specifically, and your availability. It is remembered, and almost nobody does it.

If you get an offer, ask for it in writing with CCNL, level, RAL, mensilità and contract type stated, and ask for a couple of days. Nobody withdraws an offer over a reasonable pause. If you are negotiating, negotiate once, on one or two points, with a reason — and if they will not move on money, remote days and training budget are frequently more flexible than the salary line.

If you are rejected, reply anyway and ask for one concrete piece of feedback. Emilia-Romagna’s software scene is genuinely small; the person who interviewed you today may be hiring somewhere else in eight months, and a graceful reply is remembered as clearly as a bitter one. Then write down, the same evening, the questions you could not answer — that list is the most accurate study plan you will ever get, and it points straight back at the chapters in this course.

Golden rules
  • Ninety seconds for "mi parli di lei", with hooks you want to be asked about.
  • Answer, mechanism, then your own example. The example is the differentiator.
  • "I don't know, and here is how I'd find out" beats bluffing, always.
  • Never criticise their stack. Same knowledge, asked as curiosity.
  • RAL is gross, over 13 or 14 months. Ask which, and ask the CCNL and livello.
  • Give a range with a reason, never a single low number.
  • Ask what reservations they have. It is the question that saves offers.
  • Write down what you could not answer. That is your study plan.

"Quali sono le sue aspettative economiche?"

Do not improvise this one — it is the question that costs the most and gets the least preparation: "Mi sono fatto un’idea guardando il mercato per un profilo PHP con la mia esperienza in zona Modena–Bologna, e mi aspetto una RAL tra X e Y. Detto questo, dipende anche dal pacchetto complessivo: livello e CCNL, se è su 13 o 14 mensilità, giorni di lavoro da remoto e buoni pasto cambiano parecchio il conto. Se avete già una fascia prevista per questa posizione, sono contento di partire da lì." A range, a reason, the correct vocabulary, and the question handed back — without ever sounding as though money is the only thing being discussed.
