⚠ What this site cannot do

Every other course here was written by somebody systematising a subject he already worked in. This one is not, and the difference is worth stating on the front page rather than burying it.

So: every claim in these chapters is checkable against a datasheet, a reference manual or a standard, and the chapter says which. But there is a hardware boundary a website cannot cross. Reading about DMA is not watching a transfer half-complete at 3 a.m. A Nucleo board costs under twenty euro, and the chapters that need one say so. Most Try it boxes deliberately run on a PC with nothing but a compiler, so the course is not gated behind owning hardware — but the ones that are gated are the ones that change what you may honestly write on a CV. Chapter 70 is about exactly that line.

The shape of the job, in one picture

The five responsibility lines in that advert are not five jobs. They are one signal path, read top to bottom: something physical happens, your board senses it, decides, drives an output, and tells the PLC what it did. Almost every chapter here sits somewhere on this picture, and the chapter numbers on it say where.

🧒 Simple

A normal program asks the computer for things: give me some memory, put this on the screen, open that file. Somebody else — the operating system — is in charge, and if your program does something silly it gets stopped and you see an error message.

Firmware is the program when nobody else is in charge. There is no operating system underneath, no error message, and nowhere for an error message to go. Your code starts a few microseconds after the power arrives, and it is still running a year later. If it does something silly, the machine does something silly — a pump keeps running, or a valve stays shut.

That is why this course spends so long on things that look small. In firmware there is no layer below you to catch the mistake.

🎓 Professional

The defining constraints are that resources are fixed at build time, timing is a correctness property rather than a performance one, and there is no supervisor. A desktop process that leaks memory gets restarted by somebody; firmware that leaks runs for eleven weeks and then stops in the field. This is why the field prefers static allocation, why worst case is the number that matters and the average is close to meaningless, and why a watchdog is standard equipment rather than an admission of defeat.

Three ideas carry most of the interview value, and the advert names none of them. volatile, because it is the one keyword that is about the compiler's assumptions rather than about your program (ch. 06). What an ISR is allowed to do, because every RTOS bug and half the data corruption in firmware traces back to it (ch. 26, ch. 42). And priority inversion, because being able to tell that story is the fastest way to show you think about time rather than throughput (ch. 41).

The fourth thing is not a concept. It is the ability to sit at a bench with a hardware engineer and work out together whether the bug is in the code or in the board. The advert asks for it in one line — collaborare con il team hardware — and Part 10 is four chapters long because on a bad week that line is the entire job.

Beyond the advert

The requirements above are what this company wrote down. Most of the chapters here are not in them, and that ratio is the finding rather than an omission. The advert asks for “programmazione in C (e C++) in ambito embedded” in eleven words; the memory map, undefined behaviour, what an ISR may do, priority inversion and the read-modify-write race are what those eleven words turn out to mean once a machine is relying on the answer. Interviewers ask about them whether the posting mentioned them or not.

The second source for this list is the market around the advert — Modena and Emilia-Romagna firmware listings collected the same day. They converge on a set this posting does not name and the next one will: bare-metal and FreeRTOS, ARM Cortex-M, Modbus RTU and TCP, CANopen, EtherCAT and PROFINET, embedded Linux with Yocto, MISRA C with static analysis, IEC 61508, Git, written test plans, and bring-up with an oscilloscope. Those chapters are marked beyond the advert because this advert did not ask; they are here because the next interview will.

Where this fits, and what is honestly here

This site is the tutorial layer. Underneath it, in other subjects, sit a deep course in course/, a reference application in src/ and the accounts service in api/.

None of those three exists for this subject yet, and docs/BUILD-STATUS-embedded.md records exactly which parts are real — chapter by chapter, including which ones are still placeholders. A placeholder here says so on its own page rather than being padded out with filler; if a chapter tells you it is not written, believe it.

Two neighbours worth knowing about. LogiFlow, the C# course, covers Modbus register maps, line deadlock and serial framing from the PC side — the application talking to a machine it does not control; this course is the same wire seen from the firmware side, where the timing and the buffer are yours. And plc-lab holds five IEC 61131-3 Structured Text programs, each ending in a deliberate breakage. Part 9 here covers only as much of the PLC world as a firmware engineer needs in order to be the device on the other end of the cable — it points at the lab rather than restating it.
