The CV and the ATS

How to claim embedded honestly when your evidence is a course and a twenty-euro board — and how to pass a keyword filter without writing anything you cannot defend at a bench with an oscilloscope on it.

From the advert

Not in this advert, and not in any advert. But this subject carries the overclaim risk more sharply than any other course on this site, for a reason worth stating plainly: in web development an inflated claim survives a conversation. In firmware the interviewer can put a board on the table.

Two readers, and only one of them is a person

🧒 Simple

Your CV is read twice. First by a program that looks for words, and only then by a human who looks for reasons. If the words are missing, the human never sees it. If the words are there but nothing behind them, the human throws it away.

So you need both, and they are different jobs.

🎓 Professional

The filter. An applicant tracking system matches literal strings. Write both forms of anything with two spellings, because it will not expand them for you: C/C++ and C, C++; RTOS and FreeRTOS; STM32 and ARM Cortex-M; Modbus RTU and Modbus TCP; seriale and UART and RS-485. Take the nouns from the advert itself — the posting is the keyword list, already written for you.

Format rules that are not superstition: a single-column layout (multi-column parses into interleaved nonsense), a PDF generated from text rather than an image, no skill graphics (“C++ ●●●○○” parses as nothing and means nothing), real dates, and no critical information inside a header, footer or text box. Send an Italian version to Italian companies; keep an English one for the multinationals.

The person. A firmware lead reads a junior CV for exactly one thing: is there something here they have actually built and can be asked about for twenty minutes? One real project beats a list of eleven technologies, every time. The list gets you past the filter; the project gets you the offer.

The honest claim, and why it is the stronger one

🧒 Simple

Say exactly what you did. “I built this, on this board, and it does this” is much harder to argue with than “expert in embedded systems” — and it is much easier to talk about for twenty minutes.

🎓 Professional

Three bands, and knowing which band a claim sits in is the skill:

What this course alone supports. That you understand the model — C and C++ semantics, the Cortex-M exception model, RTOS design and its failure modes, protocol structure, how a PLC sees the world. That is real, and it is worth saying as “solid theoretical grounding, systematically studied”. It is not worth saying as experience.

What a board adds, and it adds a lot. A Nucleo, a breadboard, a USB-serial adapter and a cheap logic analyser is well under €50 and moves you from the first band to the second. Once you have blinked an LED from bare registers, received on a UART with DMA and idle-line detection, run three FreeRTOS tasks and answered a Modbus poll from a PC, you can write “progetto personale” and defend every word of it.

What only a job gives you. Bring-up of a board nobody has run before. A product that shipped. A field return diagnosed from a log. Certification. Do not imply any of these, and do not let a bullet's phrasing imply them either — “sviluppo firmware per sistemi di raffreddamento industriali” reads as employment, and a recruiter will read it that way.

The section heading does the disambiguation for free. Under PROGETTI PERSONALI, a strong bullet is unambiguous and needs no hedging: 

PROGETTI PERSONALI

Nodo Modbus RTU su STM32 (progetto personale, 2026)          [repo]
- Firmware in C su STM32 (Nucleo-F411), FreeRTOS: 3 task, coda comandi,
  watchdog indipendente.
- Ricezione UART in DMA con rilevamento di linea idle per il framing a
  3,5 caratteri; gestione del driver enable RS-485 sul flag di
  trasmissione completata.
- Slave Modbus RTU: FC 03/06/16, eccezioni, mappa registri documentata
  con convenzione di indirizzamento e ordine delle word dichiarati.
- Logica di parsing compilata e testata anche su PC (Unity), senza board.

Every line there is a question you want to be asked, and every one has a chapter behind it. That is the test for a bullet: would I be glad if they asked about this? If not, cut it — a CV full of numbers you cannot defend is worse than a vague one, because every noun on it is now a question.

⚠ Trap

The self-assessment table is where honest people accidentally lie. Writing “C++: avanzato” costs you nothing to type and a great deal to defend, because “avanzato” invites template metaprogramming questions you did not want. And “RTOS” on a line by itself will be read as “has shipped an RTOS product”.

Drop the levels entirely and let the project section carry the evidence. If a form forces a level, be one notch more conservative than you feel — being revised upwards in an interview is pleasant, and being revised downwards ends it.

A related and specific one: do not list STM32 without having opened CubeIDE. It is the advert's one hard technical gate, and the first follow-up will be something concrete about the .ioc file, code regeneration overwriting your edits, or HAL versus LL (chapter 30). That is a five-minute question to survive if you have used the tool and an unsurvivable one if you have not.

Try it. The CV builder on this site produces a CV in the shape described here and then checks it against these rules — which is the part reading cannot do for you at 23:00 the night before an application closes.

1. Open cv.html and fill it in. Then do the audit by hand:

2. For every noun on the page, write one of:
      D  I could be questioned on this for 20 minutes and enjoy it
      S  I could survive 5 minutes
      X  I would be caught

   Delete every X. Every one. An X is not a stretch, it is a trap you
   set for yourself, and in this field it is sprung with hardware.

3. For every S, decide: do the work that makes it a D, or cut it. Most
   S entries become D entries with one evening and a Nucleo board --
   which is the cheapest upgrade available to you anywhere in this
   course.

4. Now open chapter 00 and find the sheet you marked at the start,
   with + / ? / - against each line of the advert. Mark it again today.
   The difference between the two sheets is the only progress
   measurement on this site that software did not generate.
Golden rules
  • Two readers: a filter that matches literal strings, then a person looking for one defensible project.
  • The advert is the keyword list. Use its nouns, in both spellings.
  • Single column, text-based PDF, no skill graphics, nothing important in a header.
  • One real project beats eleven listed technologies.
  • The test for a bullet: would you be glad to be asked about it? If not, cut it.
  • Put personal work under PROGETTI PERSONALI so the phrasing cannot imply employment.
  • Drop self-assessment levels. “Avanzato” is a promise you did not mean to make.
  • Never list STM32 without having opened the tool. It is the advert's one hard gate.
  • Under €50 of hardware moves you from “has studied” to “has built”.

“Vedo che ha esperienza su STM32. Mi racconti cosa ha fatto.”

“È un progetto personale, non lavoro: lo dico subito perché sia chiaro il contesto. Ho fatto uno slave Modbus RTU su una Nucleo-F411, in C, con FreeRTOS: tre task, una coda per i comandi e il watchdog indipendente. La parte interessante è stata la ricezione: invece di un interrupt per byte uso il DMA con il rilevamento di linea idle della UART, così il framing a 3,5 caratteri me lo fa l’hardware e ho un interrupt per frame. L’altra cosa che mi ha fatto perdere una sera è stato il driver enable dell’RS-485: lo rilasciavo sul flag di buffer vuoto e troncavo l’ultimo carattere — il master vedeva errori di CRC ogni tanto. Va rilasciato sulla trasmissione completata. Ho anche separato il parsing dal driver, così la logica la compilo e la testo sul PC senza board. Quello che non ho fatto è il bring-up di una scheda nuova né un prodotto in campo, ed è esattamente la parte che vorrei imparare qui.”
