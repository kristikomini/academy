The job posting, and the boundary of this course

One advert from Formigine, read closely. What each line is really asking for, which of them are filters and which are invitations, and the one thing a website cannot give you — said here, on the first page, rather than discovered in an interview.

From the advert

This chapter is the whole posting. Every other chapter quotes one line of it, or says plainly that it is answering something the advert never asked.

The posting

🧒 Simple

A company near Modena makes the parts that keep electrical equipment cool with liquid rather than with fans. They want somebody to write the software that runs inside their own electronic boards, and to make those boards talk to the machines around them.

The important word in the advert is formazione — training. They are not looking for somebody who already knows everything here. They are looking for somebody they can teach, which means the interview is about how you think, not about what you have memorised.

🎓 Professional

The role is Embedded Software Engineer, reporting to a Responsabile di Reparto, in a company producing components and systems for liquid cooling of electrical and electronic devices. The candidate is “inserito in un percorso di formazione” and will work “sia su firmware di sistemi embedded, che su software PLC”.

Read structurally, that last clause is the most informative sentence in the document. It says the job straddles two worlds that are usually separate: the firmware world of C on a microcontroller, and the PLC world of IEC 61131-3 on an industrial controller. Very few candidates are comfortable in both. That is the gap the role exists to fill, and it is also the gap this course is aimed at.

The conditions: Formigine (MO), full time 08.30–17.30 Monday to Friday, CCNL Metalmeccanica Aziende Industriali with 13 mensilità, inquadramento Impiegato, RAL 26–32K, initial fixed-term contract or apprendistato, no direct reports. Chapter 71 unpacks what each of those terms actually commits you to.

The five responsibility lines, and what each one hides

🧒 Simple

The advert lists five things you would do. They look like five different jobs. They are not — they are five points along one path that a signal takes: a sensor notices something, the board decides what to do, the board drives something, and then it tells the big machine controller what happened.

🎓 Professional

Taken one at a time, with what each implies that it does not say:

“Sviluppare e ottimizzare firmware in C/C++ su microcontrollori e sistemi operativi Real-Time (RTOS).” The verb ottimizzare is the tell. You will be handed code that works and asked to make it fit, or make it meet a deadline it currently misses. That is a measurement skill before it is a coding skill — chapter 43. The phrase real-time means predictable, not fast; getting that distinction wrong in an interview is expensive (chapter 37).

“Progettare l’architettura software per l’ambiente di sviluppo STM32.” Architettura in a firmware context means layering, driver interfaces and where the seams go — specifically, whether your logic can be compiled and tested on a PC without a board attached (chapter 35). It also quietly implies the HAL-versus-registers question, which has a real answer and a wrong confident one (chapter 30).

“Implementare e testare protocolli di comunicazione seriale e di rete.” Note e testare. Writing a protocol handler is the easy half; proving it survives a truncated frame, a wrong CRC, a peer that stops mid-message and a cable pulled out under load is the half that takes the time (chapter 47).

“Configurare l’interfaccia e la comunicazione tra schede elettroniche proprietarie e l’ecosistema dell’automazione industriale (PLC, sensori, attuatori).” Schede proprietarie means their own board designs, so there will be no community forum post about your problem. The parenthesis is the whole of Part 9.

“Collaborare con il team hardware per il debug e la validazione dei sistemi.” There is a hardware team, they are in the building, and you will be at a bench with them. This is the line that most software candidates underrate and that decides whether the first six months go well (chapter 63).

⚠ Trap

“Conoscenza approfondita dei protocolli di comunicazione seriale e Ethernet” reads like one requirement and is two very different ones. Seriale here means UART over RS-485 with a protocol like Modbus RTU on top, where you own the timing down to the inter-character gap. Ethernet means a TCP/IP stack, a socket API, and somebody else's switch and firewall in the middle.

A candidate who has only done one of the two, and answers as though they are the same skill, gets found out in about two follow-up questions. If you have done neither yet, say which one you would start with and why — that answer is available to you after Parts 7 and 8, and it is a better answer than a confident blur.

Which lines are filters, and which are invitations

🧒 Simple

Some lines in a job advert are there to keep people out. Others are there to describe what you will learn once you are in. Telling them apart is the difference between applying and not applying.

🎓 Professional

The degree line is written to be wide. “Laurea in Ingegneria Elettronica, Informatica, dell’Automazione, Meccatronica o background tecnico equivalente” lists four faculties and then adds an escape clause. A company that wanted only electronics graduates would have written only electronics. Chapter 68 is about reading that line correctly and about what equivalente means in practice.

The STM32 line is written to be narrow, and then softened. “Esperienza (anche accademica o di stage) su ambiente di sviluppo STM32” is the one hard technical gate in the posting — but the parenthesis explicitly admits university and internship work. A personal project on a Nucleo board is not named, and is not excluded either. What makes it count is being able to talk about it the way chapter 70 describes.

The soft-skill lines are not filler. Predisposizione al problem solving in a firmware team has a concrete meaning — a method for cornering a bug that reproduces once an hour — and it is tested with a scenario question, not a personality question (chapter 69).

The English line is a working requirement, not a formality. The reference manual for your part is 1,500 pages of English, the errata is English, and the supplier call when a part goes end-of-life is in English (chapter 67).

What this course cannot do

🧒 Simple

You can learn a great deal about firmware by reading and by writing code on a normal computer. But some of this job only exists when there is a real board on a real desk with real wires, and no website can hand you that.

🎓 Professional

Stated precisely, so you can plan around it rather than discover it:

What this course can do. Everything conceptual and everything textual. C and C++ semantics, the memory model, undefined behaviour, the exception and interrupt model of a Cortex-M, RTOS design and its failure modes, protocol structure and framing, the PLC's model of the world, and the whole of Part 11. Most Try it boxes here compile and run on a PC with nothing but gcc, deliberately — a course that is gated behind buying hardware is a course most people never start.

What it cannot do. Bring-up. The feel of a board that enumerates but does not run, a solder joint that works when warm, a scope probe with a ground lead too long to show you the real edge. Reading chapter 27 is not the same as watching a circular DMA buffer half-complete at the wrong moment. Those chapters say so at the top, and they tell you the smallest thing you could buy to fix it — which as of 2026 is a Nucleo board for under twenty euro plus, eventually, a cheap logic analyser.

Why this is on page one. Because the honest version of your claim is worth more than the inflated one, and because in this field the inflated one is caught quickly: the interviewer can put a board on the table. Chapter 70 is about saying exactly what you have done, in a way that is both true and strong.

Try it. Before reading Part 1, print the advert and mark every line with one of three symbols. This takes ten minutes and it changes how you read the next seventy chapters.

  +   I can already defend this in an interview today
  ?   I know the words but could not answer a follow-up
  -   I have never done this

Then count the '?' marks. That number is the honest one: '?' is what an
interviewer converts into '-' with a single "and what happens if...".

Keep the sheet. Chapter 70 asks you to do this again at the end, and the
difference between the two sheets is the only progress measurement on this
site that is not generated by software.
Golden rules
  • The five responsibility lines are one signal path, not five jobs: sense, decide, drive, report.
  • “Percorso di formazione” means the interview tests how you think, not what you have memorised.
  • Real-time means predictable, not fast. Getting this backwards is the most expensive single error in an embedded interview.
  • “Seriale e Ethernet” is two skills, not one, and answering as though it is one is caught in two follow-up questions.
  • The degree line is deliberately wide; the STM32 line is the real gate, and its parenthesis admits academic and internship work.
  • Name your hardware boundary before someone finds it. In this field the interviewer can put a board on the table.

“Ci racconti perché ha risposto proprio a questo annuncio.”

“Per la frase ‘sia su firmware di sistemi embedded, che su software PLC’. Sono due mondi che di solito restano separati: chi scrive firmware in C spesso non legge il ladder, e chi programma PLC non scrive C. A me interessa proprio il punto in cui si parlano — la scheda che deve dire al PLC cosa sta succedendo, e cosa fa la macchina se la scheda smette di rispondere. Poi ho letto che si tratta di un percorso di formazione: significa che vi interessa come ragiono, non quante sigle so elencare. Sul mio livello sono trasparente: le basi di C e l’architettura software le ho solide, su STM32 e RTOS ho lavorato per conto mio su una Nucleo, e il debug con oscilloscopio a fianco di un hardwarista è esattamente la parte che voglio imparare da voi.”
