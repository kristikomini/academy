Rebalancing, and the pause everyone blames on the network

What triggers a rebalance, why the classic protocol stops the whole group, and how a slow message handler produces a throughput collapse that looks like a broker problem and is not.

From the advert

Not in any advert. And the single most common cause of “Kafka is slow” reports that turn out to be an application problem.

What triggers one

🧒 Simple

Whenever the membership of a group changes, the partitions have to be handed out again. That is a rebalance.

It happens when a consumer joins, leaves, or is presumed dead — and also when the topic gains partitions.

🎓 Professional

Triggers: a member joins or leaves cleanly, a member fails to heartbeat within session.timeout.ms, a member exceeds max.poll.interval.ms, or the subscribed topics’ partition count changes.

Two of those are under your control and are the ones that cause trouble. A rolling deploy restarts every pod, so an N-pod deployment triggers up to 2N rebalances unless you use static membership (chapter 11). And a slow handler exceeding max.poll.interval.ms looks exactly like a crash to the coordinator, so the group rebalances while the consumer is still working.

The stop-the-world cost

🧒 Simple

In the classic protocol, every consumer gives up all its partitions, waits, and is then given a new set. For that whole window, nobody is consuming anything.

🎓 Professional

Eager rebalancing revokes all assignments across the whole group, then reassigns. Consumption stops group-wide for the duration — typically seconds, and much longer for stateful consumers that must rebuild local state (chapter 26) after being handed different partitions.

Worse, it can loop. If the underlying cause is a slow handler, the evicted consumer rejoins, triggers another rebalance, gets partitions, is slow again, and is evicted again. The symptom is a consumer group that never makes progress while every broker metric looks healthy — and the logs say “Attempt to heartbeat failed since group is rebalancing” over and over.

⚠ Trap

The instinct is to raise session.timeout.ms. That treats the symptom: the group tolerates the slow consumer for longer, and failure detection gets correspondingly slower. The setting that actually governs a slow handler is max.poll.interval.ms, and the real fix is usually to reduce max.poll.records so a batch finishes in reasonable time.

Cooperative rebalancing

🧒 Simple

The newer protocol only moves the partitions that actually need to move, and leaves everyone else working.

🎓 Professional

CooperativeStickyAssignor performs incremental rebalancing: it computes the new assignment, revokes only the partitions that must change hands, and lets consumers keep processing everything else throughout. For a group of ten where one member leaves, that is a handful of partitions moving instead of all of them.

It is the default in modern clients and there is essentially no reason not to use it. If you are migrating an older application, note that the upgrade is a two-step rolling change — clients configured with both assignors first, then cooperative only — because the two protocols cannot be mixed arbitrarily in one group.

Golden rules
  • Membership change means reassignment. Joins, leaves, timeouts, partition-count changes.
  • Eager rebalancing stops the whole group. Longer still for stateful consumers.
  • A slow handler looks exactly like a crash. That is the rebalance loop.
  • max.poll.interval.ms governs processing time, not session.timeout.ms.
  • Reduce max.poll.records before raising any timeout.
  • CooperativeSticky, plus static membership for rolling deploys.

“Il gruppo continua a ribilanciare e non avanza. Da dove parte?”

“Nei log cerco i messaggi di rebalance continui — ‘group is rebalancing’ ripetuto — perché quello è un ciclo, non un evento. La causa più probabile è un handler che supera max.poll.interval.ms: il coordinator lo considera morto mentre sta ancora lavorando, ribilancia, lui rientra e ricomincia. La correzione giusta è far finire il batch in tempo — di solito abbassando max.poll.records o spostando il lavoro lento fuori dal thread di poll — non alzare session.timeout.ms, che rallenta soltanto il rilevamento dei guasti veri. E mi assicuro che l’assignor sia cooperative, così un ribilanciamento non ferma tutto il gruppo.”
