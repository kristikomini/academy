The English the advert means

Several of these postings are written in English, and one asks for it explicitly. What that means in practice is narrow and learnable — and the technical vocabulary of this subject is already English, which works in your favour.

From the advert

“Excellent knowledge of English language for international work environments”.

What level they need

🧒 Simple

They need you to follow a call, be understood, and write clearly. An accent is fine. Perfect grammar is not the requirement.

🎓 Professional

B2 in practice: follow a technical discussion at natural speed, express disagreement, write documentation someone can act on. The failure companies screen for is not imperfect grammar — it is going silent in a call because you did not understand and did not say so.

Which gives the most useful habit: ask for repetition without apologising. “Sorry, could you repeat that?” and “Just to confirm — you mean X?” are ordinary professional sentences native speakers use constantly. Using them reads as competence.

The vocabulary is already English

🧒 Simple

Almost every technical word in this course stays English in an Italian conversation: topic, partition, offset, consumer group, lag.

So you are further along than you think.

🎓 Professional

Italian developers say il topic, la partizione (this one does translate), l’offset, il consumer group, il lag, fare il replay. Translating them — lo scarto for lag, il registro for the log — marks you instantly as someone who learned the subject from a textbook rather than from a codebase.

The phrases that carry a technical call are worth learning as fixed units, because under pressure you want them to cost nothing: “I see it differently — my concern is…”, “Let me check and come back to you”, “It is taking longer than I estimated; the reason is X; I expect it by Thursday”, “This is a suggestion, not a blocker”.

⚠ Trap

Two false friends that cause real confusion. Eventually means alla fine, not eventualmente — say possibly or if needed. And actually means in realtà, not attualmente; “actually we use Kafka 3.6” sounds like a correction rather than a statement of fact.

Written English is most of it

🧒 Simple

Most of your English will be written: pull requests, tickets, chat, and schema documentation. Short sentences are better English, and easier to write.

🎓 Professional

Conclusion first, then detail — international teams skim. Short sentences and simple tenses are what good technical writing looks like in any language, not a limitation.

And for asynchronous work across time zones, state what you need and by when, because a reply may be twelve hours away. “Could you confirm whether the schema change is backward compatible, so I can deploy tomorrow morning?” saves a day over “What do you think?”

Golden rules
  • B2: follow, be understood, write clearly. The accent is not the problem.
  • Ask for repetition without apologising. Silence is what they screen for.
  • The technical vocabulary stays English. Translating it marks you as bookish.
  • Learn fixed phrases for disagreeing, buying time and reporting delay.
  • Eventually ≠ eventualmente; actually ≠ attualmente.
  • Conclusion first. Say what you need and by when.

“How would you describe your English level?”

Answer in English, briefly, with evidence rather than a number: “I would say B2. I read technical documentation and follow calls without difficulty, and I write pull requests and schema documentation in English. Speaking is where I am least practised — I sometimes need a moment to find a word — but I have no problem asking someone to repeat something, and I would rather do that than pretend I understood.” Naming the weaker skill and how you handle it is more convincing than claiming C1.
