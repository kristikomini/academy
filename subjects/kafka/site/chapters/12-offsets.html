Offsets: committing, resetting and replaying

The offset is the whole consumer contract: a number saying how far you have got. Where it is stored, when it moves, and why the default setting for moving it is wrong for most workloads.

From the advert

Not in any advert. Auto-commit is on by default and is wrong for nearly everything, which makes this the highest-value chapter in Part 3.

What an offset is, and where it lives

🧒 Simple

Every record in a partition has a position number. A consumer group remembers, per partition, the position it has reached — its committed offset.

That number is stored in Kafka itself, in a special internal topic.

🎓 Professional

Offsets are per (group, topic, partition) and live in the internal compacted topic __consumer_offsets — not in ZooKeeper, which has not been true since 0.9 (chapter 05). Because it is compacted, only the latest offset per key is retained.

Distinguish two numbers that people conflate. The position is where the consumer will read next in memory; the committed offset is what has been durably recorded for the group. Only the committed one matters after a crash or a rebalance — consumption resumes from there, not from where the process happened to be.

Why auto-commit is wrong

🧒 Simple

By default the consumer commits your position every few seconds on a timer — whether or not you have actually finished processing those records.

So a crash can lose records, or repeat them, depending on the timing.

🎓 Professional

enable.auto.commit=true with auto.commit.interval.ms=5000 commits during poll(), based on time rather than on your success. Two failure shapes follow. If the commit lands before processing completes and the process then dies, those records are never reprocessed — silent loss. If it has not committed yet, they are reprocessed — duplicates, which is fine if you are idempotent (chapter 16) and not if you are not.

Set enable.auto.commit=false and commit deliberately after the work succeeds. commitSync() blocks and retries, which is the right default; commitAsync() is faster and does not retry, so the common pattern is async in the loop and a final sync in the shutdown path.

⚠ Trap

You commit the offset of the next record to read, not the one you just processed. Committing record.offset() instead of record.offset() + 1 makes every restart reprocess exactly one record per partition — a duplicate slow enough and rare enough that it survives testing and shows up as a data anomaly months later.

auto.offset.reset, and replaying on purpose

🧒 Simple

When a group has no stored position — it is brand new, or its offsets expired — Kafka has to decide where to start: the beginning, or only new records.

And you can move that position yourself, which is how you reprocess history.

🎓 Professional

auto.offset.reset applies only when there is no valid committed offset. latest (the default) means a new consumer sees only records produced after it started — which is why a freshly deployed service appears to receive nothing. earliest reads the whole retained log. Choose deliberately: for a new projection you want earliest; for a notifier you almost certainly want latest, or you will email everyone about last month.

Replay is then an operation, not a feature request: kafka-consumer-groups.sh --reset-offsets can move a group to the earliest offset, a timestamp, or a specific position — with the group stopped. That is the capability chapter 01 promised, and the reason to choose a log.

Try it. Inspect a group, then reset it to a point in time and watch it reprocess. Note the --dry-run: always look before you move a production group.

# where is this group, and how far behind?
kafka-consumer-groups.sh --bootstrap-server localhost:9092 \
  --describe --group billing

# replay from a point in time (group must be stopped)
kafka-consumer-groups.sh --bootstrap-server localhost:9092 \
  --group billing --topic orders.placed \
  --reset-offsets --to-datetime 2026-09-01T00:00:00.000 --dry-run

# ...then --execute instead of --dry-run
# other targets: --to-earliest  --to-latest  --shift-by -1000  --to-offset 42
Golden rules
  • Offsets are per group, per partition, in __consumer_offsets.
  • Only the committed offset survives a crash. The in-memory position does not.
  • Auto-commit commits on a timer, not on success. Turn it off.
  • Commit offset + 1. Off by one means a permanent duplicate per restart.
  • auto.offset.reset only applies when there is no committed offset.
  • Replay is --reset-offsets with the group stopped. Dry-run first.

“Come gestisce i commit degli offset?”

“Disattivo l’auto-commit e committo esplicitamente dopo aver elaborato, perché l’auto-commit committa a tempo e non sul successo: se committa prima che io abbia finito e il processo muore, quei record non li rielabora nessuno — è una perdita silenziosa. Committo con commitSync dopo il batch, e comunque scrivo l’handler idempotente, perché anche così la semantica resta at-least-once: se muoio dopo aver elaborato e prima di committare, quei record tornano. L’offset che committo è quello del prossimo record, non dell’ultimo elaborato.”
