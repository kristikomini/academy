Confluent, Apache, Redpanda and the managed options

What is open, what is licensed, what the managed services change, and whether the certification is worth taking. Useful because an interviewer will ask which you have used, and the honest answer depends on knowing the difference.

From the advert

“relevant certifications like Confluent Certified Developer for Apache Kafka”, from the aggregated requirement lines.

Apache, Confluent, Redpanda

🧒 Simple

Apache Kafka is the open-source project. Confluent packages it with extra components, some free and some licensed. Redpanda is a different implementation that speaks the same protocol.

🎓 Professional

Apache Kafka is Apache 2.0 — broker, clients, Streams and Connect all included. Confluent Platform adds Schema Registry and REST Proxy (Community License), ksqlDB, and enterprise features under a commercial licence: RBAC, tiered storage, cluster linking, the control centre. The important practical point is that the Schema Registry is not part of Apache Kafka — it is a Confluent component, and its licence is a real consideration for some organisations, which is why alternatives like Apicurio exist.

Redpanda reimplements the protocol in C++ with no JVM and no ZooKeeper, using a thread-per-core design. It claims lower latency and simpler operations, and it is wire-compatible so existing clients work unchanged. It is a legitimate choice; the trade is a smaller ecosystem and less operational precedent.

Managed services

🧒 Simple

You can run Kafka yourself or pay someone to run it. The main thing you buy is not having to learn broker operations.

🎓 Professional

Confluent Cloud is the most complete — the registry, Connect and ksqlDB managed alongside the brokers — and the most expensive. Amazon MSK runs Apache Kafka on your AWS account, cheaper and less managed: you still handle topic design, client tuning and often the registry. Aiven and equivalents sit in between and are common in European companies for data-residency reasons.

The honest framing: managed Kafka removes broker operations, not Kafka operations. You still own partition sizing, key design, consumer lag, schema evolution and retention — which is everything in this course. Being able to say that shows you understand where the work actually is.

⚠ Trap

Managed pricing is usually per GB written, read and stored, so a topic read by six consumer groups is billed six times for egress. A design decision that is free on your own cluster — adding another consumer group, or lengthening retention — has a direct monthly cost on a managed one, and that surprises teams migrating.

The certification

🧒 Simple

The Confluent developer certification exists and one advert mentions it. It is a signal, not a substitute for understanding.

🎓 Professional

The CCDAK covers roughly this course’s Parts 1–6: the log, producers and consumers, delivery semantics, Streams basics, schemas. It is a multiple-choice exam, and preparing for it forces coverage of the areas people skip — which is a genuine benefit.

Whether it is worth it depends on your position. For someone with no Kafka on their CV, it is an external signal that gets past a screening filter and is worth the cost. For someone with production experience it adds little. Since the adverts list it under a completamento del profilo rather than requisiti (chapter 00), treat it as a tie-breaker: useful, not decisive, and no substitute for being able to answer the questions in chapters 12 to 16.

Golden rules
  • Schema Registry is Confluent, not Apache. Its licence is a real consideration.
  • Redpanda is wire-compatible — no JVM, no ZooKeeper, smaller ecosystem.
  • Managed removes broker operations, not Kafka operations.
  • Managed pricing bills egress per consumer group. Another group has a monthly cost.
  • CCDAK is a screening signal, worth more the less Kafka experience you have.

“Ha usato Confluent o Apache Kafka?”

Answer precisely, because the difference is a real one: “Ho lavorato con Apache Kafka più lo Schema Registry, che tecnicamente è un componente Confluent e non fa parte del progetto Apache — è una distinzione che conta, perché ha una licenza sua e in alcune aziende è un tema. Di Confluent Platform conosco anche ksqlDB e il REST Proxy. Sui servizi gestiti la cosa che terrei presente è che tolgono l’operatività dei broker ma non quella di Kafka: il dimensionamento delle partizioni, la scelta delle chiavi, il lag e l’evoluzione degli schemi restano miei.”
