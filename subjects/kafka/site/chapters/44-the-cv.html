The CV and the ATS

Claiming Kafka honestly when your experience is a course and a local cluster. This subject carries a risk the others do not: it is easy to list and hard to defend, and the interviewer is often the person who runs it.

From the advert

Not in any advert — it is the gate before the advert’s requirements are discussed.

Getting past the screening

🧒 Simple

Many companies run your CV through software that extracts the text and matches it against the advert. If the text cannot be read, or the words are not there, no human sees it.

🎓 Professional

Single-column layout, real text rather than an image, standard headings, and the technologies written the way the advert writes them — “Apache Kafka”, not “Kafka (basics)”. Verify the extracted text layer rather than assuming it: a container styled for a photo can silently reorder it so the document no longer begins with your name.

The legitimate technique is alignment, not stuffing: use the advert’s vocabulary for things you have genuinely done. Hidden text and invisible keyword blocks are detectable, and being caught is not a lower score — it is disqualification.

Claiming Kafka honestly

🧒 Simple

Do not write “Apache Kafka” in a skills list with nothing behind it. Write what you actually did, and it becomes stronger rather than weaker.

🎓 Professional

A bare skills-list entry invites the question “what did you do with it?” with nothing prepared. A specific line answers it in advance and is far more convincing: “Built an event-driven flow on Kafka: 4 topics, Avro with Schema Registry, an idempotent consumer keyed on event id, replay from a chosen offset.”

Every noun there is defensible after this course, and each one invites a question you can answer. That is the test for anything on a CV: would I be happy if they asked about this for five minutes? If not, cut it — a shorter CV you can defend beats a longer one you cannot.

⚠ Trap

Kafka is the item most likely to be interviewed by someone who operates it in production, and the follow-up questions are specific: what were your partitions keyed on, what happened when a consumer fell behind, how did you handle duplicates. An inflated claim collapses at the second question — and the damage is not the gap itself, it is that everything else on the page now needs verifying.

The project that carries it

🧒 Simple

A small system somebody can look at beats any number of course certificates.

🎓 Professional

Build something small and complete: a producer, two consumer groups reading the same topic, an idempotent handler with a deduplication table, a schema in the registry, a dead-letter topic, and a README that explains the decisions. Run it with Docker Compose so it starts with one command.

That project demonstrates precisely the things the adverts cannot verify and the interview will probe: that you chose a key deliberately, that you understood at-least-once delivery, and that you know what a DLT is for. Describe it as engineering — problem, decision, trade-off — rather than as a tutorial you completed.

Golden rules
  • Single column, real text, the advert’s own words. Alignment, never stuffing.
  • Verify the extracted text layer. Layout changes reorder it silently.
  • Never a bare “Apache Kafka” in a skills list. Say what you built.
  • The test: would you be happy discussing this line for five minutes?
  • Kafka is often interviewed by whoever operates it. The follow-ups are specific.
  • One small complete system beats certificates.

“Mi parli di un progetto in cui ha usato Kafka.”

Structure it as problem, decision, trade-off — and be exact about the scale: “È un progetto mio, non di produzione, e lo dico subito. Ho costruito un flusso event-driven: un producer, due consumer group che leggono lo stesso topic per mostrare il disaccoppiamento, Avro con Schema Registry, e un consumer idempotente che inserisce l’event id con un vincolo di unicità nella stessa transazione del lavoro. La decisione di cui parlerei è la chiave: ho usato l’orderId, perché mi serve l’ordine per singolo ordine e non globale — e questo mi lascia parallelizzare. Il compromesso è che se un cliente concentrasse gran parte del traffico avrei una partizione calda.”
