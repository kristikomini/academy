Security: TLS, SASL and ACLs

Encrypting the wire, authenticating clients, and authorising topics. A cluster with no ACLs lets any client read every topic — which is a compliance problem in exactly the banking and public administration work these Milano adverts describe.

From the advert

Not in any advert. And unavoidable in the sectors those postings are drawn from, where an auditor will ask who can read a topic and “anyone” is not an answer.

Three separate concerns

🧒 Simple

Encryption stops someone reading the traffic. Authentication establishes who a client is. Authorisation decides what that client may do.

They are configured separately, and having one does not give you the others.

🎓 Professional

TLS encrypts client–broker and broker–broker traffic. SASL authenticates: SASL/PLAIN (credentials, only ever over TLS), SASL/SCRAM (salted challenge–response, the usual choice), SASL/GSSAPI (Kerberos, common in Italian banks and public administration), SASL/OAUTHBEARER. mTLS authenticates with the client certificate instead. ACLs authorise a principal for an operation on a resource.

The failure people make is stopping after the first two. TLS plus SASL means every authenticated client can still read and write every topic, because allow.everyone.if.no.acl.found defaults to true — so a cluster with authentication and no ACLs is a cluster where any service can read payroll.

ACLs in practice

🧒 Simple

An ACL says: this principal may do this operation on this resource. Give each application only what it needs.

🎓 Professional

The model is (principal, operation, resource, host), with resources being topics, consumer groups, the cluster and transactional ids. Two things catch people: a consumer needs Read on the topic and on its consumer group, and a transactional producer needs Write on its transactional.id as well. Missing either produces an authorisation error that names a resource people do not expect.

Use prefixed ACLs (--resource-pattern-type prefixed) so a team owns sales.* rather than needing a rule per topic, and set allow.everyone.if.no.acl.found=false deliberately — knowing that turning it off on a running cluster denies everything not yet granted, so it is a migration rather than a switch.

Try it. Grant exactly what one consumer needs. The second command is the one people forget, and its absence produces a confusing error.

# producer: write to its own topic
kafka-acls.sh --bootstrap-server localhost:9092 --add \
  --allow-principal User:orders-service \
  --operation Write --operation Describe \
  --topic orders.placed

# consumer: read the topic AND its group — both are required
kafka-acls.sh --bootstrap-server localhost:9092 --add \
  --allow-principal User:billing-service \
  --operation Read --operation Describe --topic orders.placed
kafka-acls.sh --bootstrap-server localhost:9092 --add \
  --allow-principal User:billing-service \
  --operation Read --group billing

# a whole domain, by prefix
kafka-acls.sh --bootstrap-server localhost:9092 --add \
  --allow-principal User:sales-team \
  --operation All --topic sales. --resource-pattern-type prefixed

What TLS does not protect

🧒 Simple

TLS protects the data while it travels. On the broker’s disk, and inside the message, it is plain.

🎓 Professional

Encryption in transit is not encryption at rest, and Kafka has no built-in message-level encryption. Anyone with access to a broker’s disk, or a backup of it, reads your records. Disk-level encryption covers the medium; for field-level protection you encrypt in the producer and decrypt in the consumer, which means key management and losing the ability to filter or join on those fields.

The GDPR point from chapter 22 lands here too: you cannot delete an individual record from a topic, so personal data either lives in a compacted topic where a tombstone can remove it, or is stored elsewhere with only a reference in the event. Being able to raise that unprompted is a strong signal in a public-administration or banking interview.

Golden rules
  • Encryption, authentication and authorisation are three settings. One does not imply another.
  • SCRAM usually; Kerberos in banks and public administration.
  • allow.everyone.if.no.acl.found defaults to true. Authentication alone protects nothing.
  • A consumer needs Read on the topic and on the group.
  • Prefixed ACLs per team, not one rule per topic.
  • TLS is not encryption at rest, and you cannot delete one record for GDPR.

“Come mettereste in sicurezza un cluster Kafka?”

“Sono tre livelli separati e servono tutti e tre. TLS per cifrare il traffico, SASL per autenticare — SCRAM di solito, Kerberos dove c’è già un dominio, tipico in banca e nella PA — e poi le ACL, che sono la parte che si dimentica: di default allow.everyone.if.no.acl.found è true, quindi un cluster autenticato ma senza ACL lascia che qualsiasi servizio legga qualsiasi topic. Le ACL le do per prefisso, un dominio per team, e ricordo che un consumer ha bisogno di Read sul topic e sul suo group. Infine TLS non è cifratura a riposo: sul disco del broker i dati sono in chiaro.”
