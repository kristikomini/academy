Consumer groups and the assignment

How partitions are shared, why more consumers than partitions is wasted, and what the assignment strategies actually change. This is the mechanism that makes Kafka scale horizontally, and the one people describe wrongly most often.

From the advert

Not in any advert. The rule that a partition goes to exactly one consumer in a group is what makes Kafka scale — and the reason an eleventh consumer on a ten-partition topic does nothing at all.

What a group is

🧒 Simple

Consumers with the same group id share the work: each partition goes to exactly one of them.

Consumers with different group ids each get everything — that is how two unrelated services read the same stream.

🎓 Professional

group.id is the unit of both scaling and independence. Within a group, partitions are divided among members so each record is processed once by that group. Across groups, every group sees every record and keeps its own offsets — which is the log model from chapter 01 made concrete.

So “how do I have two services consume the same events?” has a one-word answer: different group.id. And “how do I process faster?” is: more instances in the same group, up to the partition count.

⚠ Trap

Reusing one group.id across two genuinely different consumers is a subtle and painful bug: they share the partition assignment, so each sees roughly half the records and neither sees all of them. Nothing errors. Group ids should name the purpose — billing, search-indexer — not the application or the environment.

Assignment strategies

🧒 Simple

When membership changes, someone has to decide who gets which partitions. There are a few rules available, and they differ in how much they shuffle.

🎓 Professional

Range assigns contiguous ranges per topic and distributes badly when the partition count is not a multiple of the member count — and consistently overloads the same members across several topics. RoundRobin spreads more evenly. Sticky minimises movement: it keeps existing assignments where it can, so a rebalance does not shuffle everything.

CooperativeSticky is the one to use, and the default in modern clients. It performs incremental rebalancing (chapter 13) rather than the stop-the-world revoke-everything protocol, which matters most for stateful consumers where reassignment means rebuilding local state.

Static membership, and rolling restarts

🧒 Simple

Normally a consumer that restarts is treated as a new member, which triggers a reshuffle. You can give it a stable identity so a quick restart does not.

🎓 Professional

group.instance.id makes a consumer a static member: on restart within session.timeout.ms, it reclaims its previous partitions without a rebalance. In Kubernetes, where a rolling deploy restarts every pod in sequence, that turns N rebalances into none.

The trade is failure detection: a genuinely dead static member is not replaced until its session times out, so its partitions stop being consumed for that window. Set the timeout to match how quickly your platform restarts a pod, and be aware you have chosen slower failover in exchange for calmer deploys.

Golden rules
  • Same group id shares the work; different group ids each get everything.
  • A partition goes to exactly one member of a group. That is the whole scaling rule.
  • Name group ids by purpose, never by application or environment.
  • Sharing a group id between two different consumers splits the stream. Silently.
  • CooperativeSticky. Incremental rebalancing, minimal movement.
  • Static membership removes rolling-restart rebalances at the cost of slower failover.

“Come farebbe leggere lo stesso topic a due servizi diversi?”

“Group id diversi. Dentro lo stesso gruppo le partizioni si dividono e ogni record è elaborato da un solo membro; con gruppi diversi ognuno riceve tutto e tiene i propri offset, che è esattamente il vantaggio del modello a log. Se invece devo solo andare più veloce, aggiungo istanze allo stesso gruppo — ma fino al numero di partizioni, perché oltre restano semplicemente inattive. E i group id li chiamo per scopo, tipo billing, non per applicazione: due consumer diversi che condividono lo stesso group id si spartiscono lo stream e nessuno dei due vede tutto.”
