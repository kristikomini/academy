Replication, ISR and leader election

Replication factor, in-sync replicas and min.insync.replicas — the settings that decide whether Kafka loses your data. Nobody advertises this and every production incident review reaches it.

From the advert

Not in any advert. The combination of acks and min.insync.replicas is the single configuration pair that determines durability, and most people who have used Kafka have never set either.

Replicas and the ISR

🧒 Simple

Each partition can be copied onto several brokers. One copy leads; the others follow, keeping up as best they can.

The copies that are genuinely keeping up are called the in-sync replicas.

🎓 Professional

replication.factor = 3 means three copies on three brokers. Followers fetch from the leader continuously; a follower that stays within replica.lag.time.max.ms is in the ISR (in-sync replica set). A follower that falls behind is removed from the ISR and can rejoin when it catches up.

The ISR is the important concept because durability guarantees are expressed in terms of it, not in terms of the replication factor. Three replicas of which two have fallen behind gives you the durability of one, and nothing tells you unless you are watching the under-replicated partitions metric (chapter 33).

acks and min.insync.replicas, together

🧒 Simple

When you send a record, you choose how much confirmation to wait for: none, the leader only, or all the in-sync copies.

And separately, the topic says how many in-sync copies must exist for a write to be accepted at all. Both settings only work together.

🎓 Professional

acks=0 is fire-and-forget: fastest, and loses data on any failure. acks=1 waits for the leader only — if the leader dies before a follower replicates, that record is gone. acks=all waits for every in-sync replica.

But acks=all alone is not durability: if the ISR has shrunk to one member, “all” means that one. min.insync.replicas=2 with acks=all is the pair that means something — the write is rejected with NotEnoughReplicasException unless at least two replicas are in sync. That rejection is the system telling you the truth instead of silently accepting a write it cannot protect.

⚠ Trap

With replication.factor=3 and min.insync.replicas=3, losing any one broker stops writes entirely. That is a common over-correction: the standard configuration is RF 3 with min ISR 2, which tolerates one failure and still guarantees two copies. Setting min ISR equal to RF trades availability for nothing.

Try it. Set the topic up properly, then stop a broker and watch a producer with acks=all keep working — and watch it start refusing if you stop a second one. That refusal is the feature.

# durable topic: 3 copies, at least 2 must be in sync to accept a write
kafka-topics.sh --bootstrap-server localhost:9092 --create \
  --topic payments --partitions 6 --replication-factor 3 \
  --config min.insync.replicas=2

# producer side (Spring / plain client)
# acks: all
# enable.idempotence: true      <- also sets acks=all, retries, max.in.flight<=5
# retries: Integer.MAX_VALUE
# delivery.timeout.ms: 120000

# who is under-replicated right now?
kafka-topics.sh --bootstrap-server localhost:9092 --describe --under-replicated-partitions

Unclean leader election

🧒 Simple

If every up-to-date copy is gone, Kafka can either wait for one to come back — staying unavailable — or promote a copy that was behind, losing whatever it had not received.

🎓 Professional

unclean.leader.election.enable=false is the default and the right choice: the partition stays unavailable until an in-sync replica returns, rather than electing an out-of-sync one and silently discarding records that were acknowledged to producers.

This is the clearest CAP trade-off in Kafka, and it is a good interview answer precisely because it is a decision rather than a fact: consistency by default, availability if you explicitly ask for it. Turning it on is defensible for a metrics topic where a gap is harmless, and indefensible for payments.

Golden rules
  • Durability is expressed in terms of the ISR, not the replication factor.
  • acks=all alone is not enough. Pair it with min.insync.replicas=2.
  • RF 3 + min ISR 2 is the standard. Min ISR = RF trades availability for nothing.
  • A rejected write is the system being honest. Prefer it to a silent one.
  • Unclean leader election off unless a gap in that topic is genuinely harmless.
  • Watch under-replicated partitions. Nothing else tells you the ISR shrank.

“Come garantisce che un messaggio non venga perso?”

Name both settings, because naming only one is the common half-answer: “Servono due cose insieme. Lato producer acks=all, così aspetto la conferma di tutte le repliche in-sync, con l’idempotenza attiva per non duplicare sui retry. Lato topic min.insync.replicas=2 con replication factor 3: senza quello, acks=all non vuol dire niente, perché se l’ISR si è ridotto a una replica ‘tutte’ è una sola. Con quella coppia, se non ci sono almeno due repliche allineate la scrittura viene rifiutata — che è il comportamento che voglio: meglio un errore che un dato accettato e non protetto.”
