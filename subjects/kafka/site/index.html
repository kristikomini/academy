The shape of the job, in one picture

Kafka is one idea with consequences. The idea is an append-only log that readers track their own position in. Partitions, offsets, consumer groups, replay and every delivery guarantee are consequences of that, not separate features to memorise.

🧒 Simple

A queue is a basket of letters. You take one out, and it is gone — nobody else can read it, and neither can you tomorrow.

Kafka is not a basket. It is a diary that only ever gets written at the end. Reading page 40 does not tear it out. Everyone who reads the diary keeps their own bookmark, so two people can read at different speeds, and anyone can move their bookmark back to page 1 and read the whole thing again. That last sentence — you can go back — is the entire reason companies choose it.

🎓 Professional

Kafka is a distributed, partitioned, replicated commit log. Consumers are not sent messages; they pull from an offset they own, which is what decouples consumer throughput from producer throughput and makes replay a normal operation rather than a recovery procedure.

Three consequences carry most of the interview value. Ordering is per-partition, so the key you choose is the ordering guarantee you get. Retention is time- or size-based, not read-based, so a message is not removed when consumed — which is why the log can be replayed and why disk sizing is a design input. And delivery is at-least-once by default, so correctness comes from idempotent consumers rather than from a broker setting.

Chapters 15, 16 and 39 are the three that decide whether a system built on this is correct. Everything else is throughput.

Beyond the advert

The requirements above are what companies write down. Twenty-nine of the forty-six chapters here are not in them — a far higher share than any other subject on this site, and that ratio is the honest finding rather than an omission. The adverts ask for “Kafka” as a single word; offsets, rebalancing, delivery semantics, the outbox pattern and idempotent consumers are what that word turns out to mean once you are responsible for a system. Interviewers ask about them whether the posting mentioned them or not, because they are what separates having used a client library from understanding the broker.
