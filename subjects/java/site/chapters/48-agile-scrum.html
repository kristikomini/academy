Agile, Scrum and the ceremonies

One advert asks for it in English, and every Italian software house will ask how you work. The useful preparation is not the Scrum Guide — it is knowing what a team actually expects of you in each meeting, and being able to describe how you worked without reciting a framework.

From the advert

“Full-time participation in projects working collaboratively with development teams in Agile Scrum methodology”.

The four meetings, and what you owe each

🧒 Simple

Stand-up: what you did, what you will do, what is blocking you. Refinement: understand the upcoming work well enough to estimate it. Planning: commit to a sprint. Retrospective: improve how the team works.

🎓 Professional

The stand-up is a synchronisation, not a status report to a manager — which is why “yesterday I worked on the ticket” is a wasted turn and “I am blocked on the test environment, who has access?” is a useful one. Refinement is where you have the most leverage: the questions that prevent a week of wrong work get asked here, before anyone estimates.

Retrospectives are the one people dismiss and the one interviewers listen for, because a candidate who can name something their team changed as a result of one has clearly been in a real team. Have that example ready.

Story points, and what estimates are for

🧒 Simple

Teams estimate in points rather than hours, comparing one task to another. The point is to plan a sprint, not to measure how fast you type.

🎓 Professional

Points express relative size — complexity, uncertainty and effort together — because people are far better at comparison than at absolute duration. Velocity is then a planning input for the team, not a productivity metric for an individual, and a team whose velocity is being compared to another team’s has an organisational problem, not an estimation one.

Two things that read as experience: saying “I do not know enough to estimate this yet, I need a spike” instead of guessing, and flagging slippage early rather than on the last day. Nobody minds a task taking longer; they mind finding out about it too late to react.

⚠ Trap

Do not present yourself as an Agile purist in an interview. Most Italian software houses run something in between Scrum and project work — two-week sprints with a fixed-date commessa underneath — and a candidate who insists on doctrine reads as someone who will be difficult about reality. Describe how you worked, then ask how they work.

What working well actually looks like

🧒 Simple

Small pull requests, clear commit messages, code review as a conversation, and asking early when you are stuck.

🎓 Professional

The habits that make you easy to work with: pull requests small enough to review properly; commit messages that say why, since the diff already says what; reviewing others’ code promptly, because a PR waiting two days blocks a person; and separating “this is wrong” from “I would have done this differently” when you comment.

And a rule worth stating out loud in an interview: ask after you are stuck, not after a day. Teams have a threshold — typically half an hour to an hour — and both failure modes are real. Asking immediately means you have not tried; silence for a day means somebody could have unblocked you in two minutes.

Golden rules
  • Stand-up is synchronisation, not a status report. Lead with blockers.
  • Refinement is where the leverage is. Ask the question before anyone estimates.
  • Points are relative size. Velocity plans a team; it does not measure a person.
  • “I need a spike” beats a guess. And flag slippage early.
  • Small PRs, and commit messages that say why.
  • Ask after half an hour stuck — not immediately, not the next day.

“Come lavorate nel suo team attuale? Usate Scrum?”

Describe reality, then ask about theirs — that exchange is the point of the question: “Sprint di due settimane, stand-up giornaliero, refinement a metà sprint e retrospettiva alla fine. La parte che trovo più utile è il refinement: è lì che si scoprono i malintesi, prima che qualcuno stimi. In retrospettiva, per esempio, abbiamo deciso di tenere le pull request sotto una certa dimensione, e i tempi di review si sono dimezzati. Voi come siete organizzati? Avete sprint fissi anche sulle commesse a data fissa?”
