The CV and the ATS

Almost every Italian software house screens before the technical interview, and candidates lose here for reasons that have nothing to do with the code. What the software reads, what the human reads, and the honest way to pass both.

From the advert

Not in any advert — it is the gate before the advert’s requirements are ever discussed.

What the screening software reads

🧒 Simple

Many companies run your CV through software that extracts the text and looks for the skills in the advert. If the text cannot be extracted, or the words are not there, a human may never see it.

🎓 Professional

An ATS parses the text layer of your PDF. That means: a single-column layout (multi-column text can be extracted in the wrong order), real text rather than an image, standard section headings, and the technologies written the way the advert writes them. If the advert says “Spring Boot” and your CV says “SpringBoot”, that is a match you have thrown away for nothing.

The legitimate technique is alignment, not stuffing: use the advert’s vocabulary for things you have genuinely done. White text, hidden keyword blocks and invisible layers are detectable, and a company that catches you doing it will not proceed — the cost is not a lower score, it is disqualification.

⚠ Trap

Check the extracted text, do not assume it. Export the PDF, extract the text layer, and read what actually comes out — a container styled for a photo can silently reorder the layer so the document no longer starts with your name. That is a real failure mode, not a hypothetical one.

What the human reads

🧒 Simple

A first pass takes well under a minute. What survives it is the top third of the first page: who you are, what you work with, and one thing you have built.

🎓 Professional

Lead with a two-line summary naming the role you want and the stack, then experience in reverse order, then education. Write bullets as outcome plus evidence, not responsibilities: “Built a REST API in Spring Boot: 34 endpoints, JWT auth, 210 tests, deployed on Kubernetes” says more than “Responsible for backend development”.

Every number is a question you have invited, so use only figures you can defend for five minutes. Two pages maximum. For Italian SMEs a photo is conventional and expected; for international and product companies leave it off, which is why keeping an Italian version and an English version is worth the effort rather than translating one at the last minute.

Projects, when experience is thin

🧒 Simple

If you do not have years of employment, a real project that someone can open is the strongest thing on the page.

🎓 Professional

A deployed, working project with a readable README beats a list of courses. It is evidence of exactly the thing an employer cannot otherwise verify: that you finish things. Link it, and make sure the link works — a dead link is worse than no link.

Describe it the way you would describe work: the problem, the decisions, the trade-offs. And be precise about what is yours; a reviewer who finds a tutorial repository presented as original work stops reading. The projects on your own machine — a .NET Clean Architecture application, a Jakarta EE service, this study platform — are better evidence than most of what appears on junior CVs, provided the README says what they demonstrate.

Golden rules
  • Single column, real text, standard headings. The parser is not clever.
  • Use the advert’s exact words for things you have really done. Alignment, never stuffing.
  • Verify the extracted text layer. Layout changes can reorder it silently.
  • Outcome plus evidence, not responsibilities.
  • Every number invites a question. Only claim what you can defend.
  • Photo for Italian SMEs, none for international. Keep both versions maintained.

“Mi parli di un progetto di cui va particolarmente fiero.”

Pick one where you made a decision you can defend, and structure it: problem, decision, trade-off, result. “Ho costruito una piattaforma di studio per prepararmi ai colloqui: sito statico senza framework, motore di ripetizione spaziata, e un servizio di account separato per la sincronizzazione. La decisione di cui vado più fiero è averla tenuta apribile da file://, senza build: significa che funziona sempre, anche offline, e il prezzo è che ho dovuto scrivere il motore a mano invece di usare un framework.” A named trade-off is what makes it sound like engineering rather than a tutorial.
