The job posting, decoded

Six real adverts, from Bologna, Modena and Milano. They look like six different jobs and they are mostly one job described six ways. This chapter reads them line by line, says what each line is actually testing, and points at the chapter that answers it — so that nothing in the rest of the course arrives without a reason.

From the advert

"the whole advert". This chapter is the map. Every other chapter in this course carries either a req — a line quoted verbatim from one of these six postings — or an extra, saying why it is here when no advert asked for it. The home page renders those as two separate tables so the claim stays checkable.

What an Italian advert is actually saying

An advert is not a specification. It is a list written by a technical lead, filtered through HR, and it has a structure worth learning to read.

🧒 Simple

Job adverts have two halves, and they are not equally serious. The first half (requisiti) is what you must have. The second half (a completamento del profilo, gradita conoscenza) is a wish list.

People fail to apply because they do not have something from the second list. That is reading the advert backwards. The second list is what they hope for, not what they need.

🎓 Professional

The requisiti block is the screening filter and usually the interview agenda: an interviewer with no time to prepare will walk down it. The a completamento del profilo block is negotiating room — it exists so the company can hire someone who has four of the eight and call it a good fit.

Two things follow. First, you should be able to speak for two minutes on every line of the first block, which is what this course is for. Second, a gap in the second block is not a reason not to apply; it is a question you should have a prepared answer to, of the form "not yet, here is the nearest thing I have done, here is how long I would need".

The lines all six had in common

🧒 Simple

Strip the six adverts down and the same four things appear in every one: the language (Java), the framework (Spring Boot), talking to a database (JPA), and building web APIs (REST).

Everything else — Kubernetes, AWS, Kafka, Angular, Quarkus — appeared in one or two of them. Learn the four that are always there first.

🎓 Professional

The intersection across all six postings is: Java (versions given as 8+, 11+ or 17), Spring / Spring Boot, Spring Data JPA, and REST API design. That intersection is Part 2 and Part 3 of this course, and it is where preparation time has the highest expected value, because it is the part that cannot be skipped by any of the six.

The union is much larger and much shallower: OpenShift and Kubernetes (one advert), AWS EC2/S3/Route53 (one), Kafka and RabbitMQ (one), MongoDB and Oracle (one), Angular and TypeScript (one), Quarkus (one), GraphQL and industrial protocols (one). Each of those is a single chapter here, deliberately — enough to hold a conversation and be honest about the boundary, which is what a mid-level interview is actually checking.

Seniority, RAL, and what “middle” means here

🧒 Simple

The adverts ask for two, three or five years. Those numbers are softer than they look: they describe the level of conversation the company expects, not a stopwatch.

RAL is the number that matters in the first phone call. It is your gross annual salary including the extra monthly instalments — not what lands in your bank account each month.

🎓 Professional

Junior, middle and senior in this market map roughly to: can complete a well-specified task; can own a feature end to end and decide small designs; can own a service and be the person others ask. The year counts in adverts are a proxy for that and are routinely stretched by candidates and by companies alike.

RAL (retribuzione annua lorda) is gross annual pay across 13 or 14 mensilità. The Esprimo Milano posting in the Kafka corpus quotes 35–38k RAL under CCNL Metalmeccanico; net is roughly 55–65 % of that depending on region and deductions. Know your own number before the call, because you will be asked for it in the first ten minutes and "I don't know" reads as inexperience.

⚠ Trap

Do not answer the RAL question with a monthly net figure. The recruiter is asking for an annual gross number and will silently convert whatever you say into one — usually to your disadvantage. Give a range, in RAL, and say it is negotiable on the whole package. Chapter 51 has the wording.

How to use this course

🧒 Simple

Read a chapter, then answer its questions. Reading feels like learning and mostly is not; the questions are the part that sticks.

Use the Simple / Pro switch as a test, not a preference. Hide the professional box, read the simple one, and try to reconstruct the other. Then check.

🎓 Professional

The scoring is deliberately biased against passive reading: opening a chapter is worth a tenth of what answering its questions is worth, because the strongest replicated finding in the learning literature is that retrieval beats review. A progress bar you can fill by scrolling teaches scrolling.

The order matters more in Parts 1 and 4 than anywhere else. Chapters 09 (concurrency), 22 (connection pools) and 25 (transactions) explain behaviour that the framework chapters otherwise present as magic — if you read those three early, Spring stops being a set of annotations to memorise.

Golden rules
  • The first block is the interview agenda. Be able to talk for two minutes on every line of it.
  • A gap in the second block is not a blocker — it is a question you should have an answer prepared for.
  • Java, Spring Boot, JPA and REST are in all six adverts. Everything else was in one or two.
  • Know your RAL before the first call, as a range, gross, annual.
  • Answer the questions. The chapters are the setup; retrieval is the mechanism.

“Ha letto l’annuncio? Quali requisiti pensa di coprire meglio?”

This is an invitation, not a test, and the mistake is modesty. Pick the two or three lines you are strongest on, name them in the advert’s own words, and attach evidence to each: “Direi Spring Boot e Spring Data JPA — ho costruito una API REST con autenticazione JWT e una trentina di endpoint, e ho lavorato parecchio sul problema dell’N+1 con le fetch join.” Then volunteer the weakest one before they find it: “Su Kubernetes sono più indietro: ho fatto il deploy dei miei progetti, ma non ho gestito un cluster.” Naming your own gap first is the single cheapest way to make the rest of your claims credible.
