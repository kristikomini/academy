The interview

Mi parli di lei, the technical round, RAL and contracts, and the questions you must ask them. The technical preparation is the other fifty chapters; this one is the twenty minutes either side of it, where a surprising number of good candidates lose the offer.

From the advert

“the whole advert”. Everything in this course arrives here.

“Mi parli di lei”

🧒 Simple

The opening question is not about your life. It is asking: why are you a sensible person for this job? Two minutes, ending on why you applied.

🎓 Professional

Structure it: where you are now, one or two things you have built that are relevant to this advert, and why this role. Rehearse it aloud — not to memorise, but so the shape is automatic and the two minutes do not become six.

Then, throughout: answer with a structure. For behavioural questions, situation → task → action → result. For technical ones, the mechanism, then the consequence, then an example — which is exactly the shape every Al colloquio box in this course uses. And when you do not know, say so and say what you would do: “I have not used that; from what I understand it does X — is that how you use it?” is a far better answer than a confident guess, because a confident wrong answer makes everything else you said suspect.

The technical round

🧒 Simple

Think out loud. They are watching how you approach a problem, not only whether you reach the answer.

🎓 Professional

Whether it is live coding, a take-home or a whiteboard design, the process is the assessment: restate the problem, ask about constraints, say what you are assuming, choose an approach and say why, then check your own work. Silence is the worst outcome — a wrong idea narrated aloud gives them something to engage with; five quiet minutes gives them nothing.

For a design question, the reliable moves are in this course: name the trade-off, ask what the traffic and consistency requirements are, and mention how you would know it was broken (chapter 35). For a take-home, tests and a README explaining your decisions are worth more than an extra feature.

⚠ Trap

Do not oversell. An inflated claim survives exactly until the follow-up question, and the damage is not the gap — it is that everything else you said now needs verifying. If Kafka is on your CV because you did this course and ran a local cluster, say precisely that. It is a perfectly good answer, and it is one they can trust.

RAL, contract, and the questions you ask

🧒 Simple

Know your number before the call, as an annual gross range. And have questions ready — having none reads as not being interested.

🎓 Professional

Give a RAL range, gross, annual, and say it is negotiable on the overall package (ticket, smart working, training budget, welfare). Ask which CCNL and which level, whether it is indeterminato or apprendistato, and how many mensilità — those change the real figure and asking is completely normal.

Your questions should be the ones you actually want answered, and the good ones reveal how the team works: “How does a change reach production?”, “What does the codebase look like — how much is legacy?”, “Who would I be working with day to day?”, “What would a good first three months look like?” The answers tell you more about the job than the advert did.

Golden rules
  • Two minutes for mi parli di lei, ending on why this role.
  • Answer with a structure — mechanism, consequence, example.
  • “I do not know, but…” beats a confident guess. Every time.
  • Think out loud. The process is what is being assessed.
  • Never oversell. An inflated claim makes your true ones suspect.
  • RAL as a gross annual range, and ask about CCNL, level and mensilità.
  • Always have questions. Ask how a change reaches production.

“Ha domande per noi?”

Never say no. Ask two or three that show you are evaluating them as well: “Sì, tre. Come arriva una modifica in produzione — avete una pipeline, e quanto spesso rilasciate? Il codice su cui lavorerei quanto è legacy e quanto nuovo? E cosa considerereste un buon primo trimestre per questa posizione?” The last one is particularly good: it makes them articulate what success looks like, and their answer tells you whether they have thought about it at all.
